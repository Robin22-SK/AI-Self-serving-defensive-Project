## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly being assessed for safety
and compliance in multi-turn caregiving conversations, yet systematic
empirical comparisons of their capabilities remain limited. This study
presents a comparative evaluation of six LLMs---GPT-5.4 pro, Grok 4,
Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5, and DeepSeek-v3---on safety
and compliance in multi-turn caregiving conversations using
InvisibleBench benchmark. We evaluated safety score across 713 test
instances. GPT-5.4 pro achieved the highest overall safety score,
significantly outperforming other models. These results underscore the
importance of model selection in assessing LLM safety and compliance in
multi-turn caregiving conversations tasks and provide a benchmark for
future research.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded their
capacity across diverse reasoning, generation, and task-execution
problems. Yet performance on safety and compliance in multi-turn
caregiving conversations cannot be inferred reliably from
general-purpose evaluations, because differences in model design,
training, prompting, and decoding can produce substantial variation on
task-specific outcomes. Existing approaches are limited by the lack of
coherent evaluation criteria, making controlled comparisons across
multiple systems difficult. To address this gap, this study evaluates
GPT-5.4 pro, Grok 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5, and
DeepSeek-v3 on safety and compliance in multi-turn caregiving
conversations using standardized safety score metrics. The results
indicate that GPT-5.4 pro achieves the strongest performance on safety
and compliance in multi-turn caregiving conversations among the six. The
study provides a reproducible framework for comparative evaluation of
LLMs on safety and compliance in multi-turn caregiving conversations.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study can be broadly categorized into three
areas: LLM benchmarking, safety and compliance evaluation in multi-turn
caregiving conversations, and benchmark design. A central challenge
identified in the literature is the development of standardized
evaluation indices that enable meaningful comparisons across different
LLMs. However, existing evaluations have often focused on comparing
different versions within the same model family, leaving the systematic
benchmarking of multiple heterogeneous models comparatively
underexplored. This study addresses this gap by conducting a controlled
comparative evaluation of six LLMs under a common evaluation framework.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance using InvisibleBench, a suite of 713
multi-turn caregiving conversation scenarios spanning three complexity
tiers, grounded in caregiver demographic statistics and scored for
crisis detection, regulatory compliance, and cultural fitness. The
benchmark requires models to identify and rank the most relevant
candidate for each instance, enabling performance evaluation across
varying retrieval depths. The dataset was partitioned into a 20%
development set for prompt tuning and an 80% test set for final
evaluation. InvisibleBench is well suited to evaluating safety and
compliance in multi-turn caregiving conversations because it provides
outputs that can be directly evaluated against validated reference
rankings using rank-aware safety score metrics.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six language models: GPT-5.4 pro, Grok 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro,
Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5, and DeepSeek-v3. All models were evaluated
under identical prompt configurations and deterministic decoding
settings (*T* = 0.0). Each model generated a ranked candidate list for
every test instance. Performance was measured using Top-5 F1-Score as a
primary metric to evaluate balanced precision and recall, while Mean
Reciprocal Rank (MRR) served as a secondary metric to measure ranking
efficiency based on the position of the first correct answer.
Statistical differences between models were assessed using McNemar\'s
test on paired binary outcome indicators (α = 0.05). Pairwise effect
sizes were calculated using Cohen's *h*, and 95% confidence intervals
were estimated using non-parametric bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied across the six models on InvisibleBench, with GPT-5.4
pro achieving the strongest overall performance across all evaluation
criteria. At the Top-5 threshold, GPT-5.4 pro recorded the highest
Accuracy. An omnibus comparison indicated a significant difference in
Top-5 Accuracy across the six models (Cochran's *Q*(5) = 38.47, *p* \<
.001, Cohen's *w* = 0.23). Post-hoc pairwise comparisons using McNemar's
tests with Holm correction further showed that GPT-5.4 pro significantly
outperformed the remaining models overall (adjusted *p* \< .05).
Relative performance rankings were generally consistent across Top-5
F1-Score thresholds, although inter-model differences narrowed as *k*
increased. GPT-5.4 pro also achieved the highest MRR (0.879), indicating
that relevant candidates were more consistently positioned near the top
of its generated rankings. Overall, GPT-5.4 pro emerged as the
strongest-performing model on safety and compliance in multi-turn
caregiving conversations, whereas the remaining models showed
comparatively similar performance profiles, with specific rankings
slightly varying across top-*k* thresholds.

## 6. Discussion

The results indicate that GPT-5.4 pro delivers the strongest performance
on safety and compliance in multi-turn caregiving conversations,
consistently outperforming the other five models across Top-5 F1-Score
evaluation thresholds and achieving the highest MRR. It further
indicates that relevant candidates were more consistently positioned
near the top of its generated rankings. This advantage may reflect
differences in model architecture, training data, instruction tuning, or
related model characteristics, although these explanations require
further testing through controlled ablation or sensitivity analyses.

Several limitations should be noted. First, the evaluation was
restricted to InvisibleBench benchmark, which may limit generalizability
across datasets and domains. Second, the fixed inference settings,
including temperature, maximum output length, and other decoding
parameters, may not optimize performance equally across models. Future
work should extend at top-*k* rank from historical or benchmark data to
new, unseen instances and examine whether the observed performance
differences remain stable under alternative prompting and inference
conditions. Overall, this study provides a standardized baseline for the
comparative evaluation of LLMs on safety and compliance in multi-turn
caregiving conversations.
