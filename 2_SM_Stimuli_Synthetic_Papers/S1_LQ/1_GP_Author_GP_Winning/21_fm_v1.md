## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) being assessed for their safety against
animal welfare reasoning under multi-turn adversarial pressure, yet
systematic empirical comparisons of their capabilities remain limited.
This study presents a comparative evaluation of six LLMs---GPT-5.4 pro,
Grok 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3, and Claude Sonnet
4.5---for their safety againt animal welfare reasoning under multi-turn
adversarial pressure using MANTA benchmark. We evaluated value stability
across 713 test instances. GPT-5.4 pro achieved the highest overall
value stability, significantly outperforming other models. These results
underscore the importance of model selection in using LLMs for animal
welfare reasoning under multi-turn adversarial pressure tasks and
provide a benchmark for future research.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded their
capacity across diverse reasoning, generation, and task-execution
problems.Yet assessment for their safety against animal welfare
reasoning under multi-turn adversarial pressure cannot be inferred
reliably from general-purpose evaluations, because differences in model
design, training, prompting, and decoding can produce substantial
variation on task-specific outcomes. Existing approaches are limited by
the lack of coherent evaluation criteria, making controlled comparisons
across multiple systems difficult. To address this gap, this study
evaluates GPT-5.4 pro, Grok 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3,
and Claude Sonnet 4.5 for their safety against animal welfare reasoning
under multi-turn adversarial pressure using standardized value stability
metrics. The results indicate that GPT-5.4 pro achieves the strongest
defence performance on animal welfare reasoning under multi-turn
adversarial pressure among the six. The study provides a reproducible
framework for comparative evaluation of LLMs for their safety against
animal welfare reasoning under multi-turn adversarial pressure.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study can be broadly categorized into three
areas: LLM benchmarking, animal welfare reasoning under multi-turn
adversarial pressure, and benchmark design. A central challenge
identified in the literature is the development of standardized
evaluation indices that enable meaningful comparisons across different
LLMs. However, existing evaluations have often focused on comparing
different versions within the same model family, leaving the systematic
benchmarking of multiple heterogeneous models comparatively
underexplored. This study addresses this gap by conducting a controlled
comparative evaluation of six LLMs under a common evaluation framework.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance using MANTA, a 713-conversation,
five-turn benchmark spanning 65 animal species, in which an explicit
welfare stance is tested against three scripted adversarial-pressure
turns. The benchmark requires models to identify and rank the most
relevant candidate for each instance, enabling performance evaluation
across varying retrieval depths. The dataset was partitioned into a 20%
development set for prompt tuning and an 80% test set for final
evaluation. MANTA is well suited to evaluating LLMs' safety against
animal welfare reasoning under multi-turn adversarial pressure because
it provides outputs that can be directly evaluated against validated
reference rankings using rank-aware value stability metrics.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six language models: GPT-5.4 pro, Grok 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro,
Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3, and Claude Sonnet 4.5. All models were evaluated
under identical prompt configurations and deterministic decoding
settings (*T* = 0.0). Each model generated a ranked candidate list for
every test instance. Performance was measured using Top-5 F1-Score as a
primary metric to evaluate balanced precision and recall, while Mean
Reciprocal Rank (MRR) served as a secondary metric to measure ranking
efficiency based on the position of the first correct answer.
Statistical differences between models were assessed using McNemar\'s
test on paired binary outcome indicators (α = 0.05). Pairwise effect
sizes were calculated using Cohen's *h*, and 95% confidence intervals
were estimated using non-parametric bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied across the six models on MANTA, with GPT-5.4 pro
achieving the strongest overall performance across all evaluation
criteria. At the Top-5 threshold, GPT-5.4 pro recorded the highest
Accuracy. An omnibus comparison indicated a significant difference in
Top-5 Accuracy across the six models (Cochran's *Q*(5) = 38.47, *p* \<
.001, Cohen's *w* = 0.23). Post-hoc pairwise comparisons using McNemar's
tests with Holm correction further showed that GPT-5.4 pro significantly
outperformed the remaining models overall (adjusted *p* \< .05).
Relative performance rankings were generally consistent across Top-5
F1-Score thresholds, although inter-model differences narrowed as *k*
increased. GPT-5.4 pro also achieved the highest MRR (0.879), indicating
that relevant candidates were more consistently positioned near the top
of its generated rankings. Overall, GPT-5.4 pro emerged as the
strongest-performing model for their safety against animal welfare
reasoning under multi-turn adversarial pressure, whereas the remaining
models showed comparatively similar performance profiles, with specific
rankings slightly varying across top-*k* thresholds.

## 6. Discussion

The results indicate that GPT-5.4 pro delivers the strongest performance
for their safety against animal welfare reasoning under multi-turn
adversarial pressure, consistently outperforming the other five models
across Top-5 F1-Score evaluation thresholds and achieving the highest
MRR. It further indicates that relevant candidates were more
consistently positioned near the top of its generated rankings. This
advantage may reflect differences in model architecture, training data,
instruction tuning, or related model characteristics, although these
explanations require further testing through controlled ablation or
sensitivity analyses.

Several limitations should be noted. First, the evaluation was
restricted to MANTA benchmark, which may limit generalizability across
datasets and domains. Second, the fixed inference settings, including
temperature, maximum output length, and other decoding parameters, may
not optimize performance equally across models. Future work should
extend at top-*k* rank from historical or benchmark data to new, unseen
instances and examine whether the observed performance differences
remain stable under alternative prompting and inference conditions.
Overall, this study provides a standardized baseline for the comparative
evaluation of LLMs for their safety against animal welfare reasoning
under multi-turn adversarial pressure.
