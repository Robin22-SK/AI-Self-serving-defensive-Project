## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly being assessed for their
safety against multimodal adversarial prompting, yet systematic
empirical comparisons of their stability remain limited. This study
presents a comparative evaluation of six LLMs---GPT-5.4 pro, Qwen-2.5,
DeepSeek-v3, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and Grok 4---for their
safety against multimodal adversarial prompting using the adversarial
red-team set benchmark. We evaluated defence success rate across 713
test instances. GPT-5.4 pro achieved the highest overall defence success
rate, significantly outperforming other models. These results underscore
the importance of model selection in assessing LLM multimodal
adversarial prompting risks and provide a benchmark for future research.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded their
capacity across diverse reasoning, generation, and task-execution
problems. Yet assessment for their safety against multimodal adversarial
prompting risks cannot be inferred reliably from general-purpose
evaluations, because differences in model design, training, prompting,
and decoding can produce substantial variation on task-specific
outcomes. Existing approaches are limited by the lack of coherent
evaluation criteria, making controlled comparisons across multiple
systems difficult. To address this gap, this study evaluates GPT-5.4
pro, Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and Grok
4 on their safety against multimodal adversarial prompting using
standardized defence success rate metrics. The results indicate that
GPT-5.4 pro achieves the strongest safety against multimodal adversarial
prompting risks among the six. The study provides a reproducible
framework for comparative evaluation of LLMs for their safety against
multimodal adversarial prompting.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study can be broadly categorized into three
areas: LLM benchmarking, multimodal adversarial prompting, and benchmark
design. A central challenge identified in the literature is the
development of standardized evaluation indices that enable meaningful
comparisons across different LLMs. However, existing evaluations have
often focused on comparing different versions within the same model
family, leaving the systematic benchmarking of multiple heterogeneous
models comparatively underexplored. This study addresses this gap by
conducting a controlled comparative evaluation of six LLMs under a
common evaluation framework.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model defence performance using the adversarial red-team
set, a benchmark of 713 prompts, half text-only and half paired
multimodal equivalents, authored by 26 professional red teamers across
three harm categories. The benchmark requires models to identify and
rank the most relevant candidate for each instance, enabling defence
performance evaluation across varying retrieval depths. The dataset was
partitioned into a 20% development set for prompt tuning and an 80% test
set for final evaluation. The adversarial red-team set is well suited to
evaluating their safety against multimodal adversarial prompting because
it provides outputs that can be directly evaluated against validated
reference rankings using rank-aware defence success rate metrics.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six language models: GPT-5.4 pro, Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3,
Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and Grok 4. All models were evaluated
under identical prompt configurations and deterministic decoding
settings (*T* = 0.0). Each model generated a ranked candidate list for
every test instance. Defence performance was measured using Top-5
F1-Score as a primary metric to evaluate balanced precision and recall,
while Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) served as a secondary metric to measure
ranking efficiency based on the position of the first correct answer.
Statistical differences between models were assessed using McNemar\'s
test on paired binary outcome indicators (α = 0.05). Pairwise effect
sizes were calculated using Cohen's *h*, and 95% confidence intervals
were estimated using non-parametric bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied across the six models on the adversarial red-team
set, with GPT-5.4 pro achieving the strongest overall defence
performance across all evaluation criteria. At the Top-5 threshold,
GPT-5.4 pro recorded the highest Accuracy. An omnibus comparison
indicated a significant difference in Top-5 Accuracy across the six
models (Cochran's *Q*(5) = 38.47, *p* \< .001, Cohen's *w* = 0.23).
Post-hoc pairwise comparisons using McNemar's tests with Holm correction
further showed that GPT-5.4 pro significantly outperformed the remaining
models overall (adjusted *p* \< .05). Relative performance rankings were
generally consistent across Top-5 F1-Score thresholds, although
inter-model differences narrowed as *k* increased. GPT-5.4 pro also
achieved the highest MRR (0.879), indicating that relevant candidates
were more consistently positioned near the top of its generated
rankings. Overall, GPT-5.4 pro emerged as the strongest-performing model
on safety against multimodal adversarial prompting, whereas the
remaining models showed comparatively similar performance profiles, with
specific rankings slightly varying across top-*k* thresholds.

## 6. Discussion

The results indicate that GPT-5.4 pro delivers the strongest safety
against multimodal adversarial prompting, consistently outperforming the
other five models across Top-5 F1-Score evaluation thresholds and
achieving the highest MRR. It further indicates that relevant candidates
were more consistently positioned near the top of its generated
rankings. This advantage may reflect differences in model architecture,
training data, instruction tuning, or related model characteristics,
although these explanations require further testing through controlled
ablation or sensitivity analyses.

Several limitations should be noted. First, the evaluation was
restricted to the adversarial red-team set benchmark, which may limit
generalizability across datasets and domains. Second, the fixed
inference settings, including temperature, maximum output length, and
other decoding parameters, may not optimize performance equally across
models. Future work should extend at top-*k* rank from historical or
benchmark data to new, unseen instances and examine whether the observed
defence performance differences remain stable under alternative
prompting and inference conditions. Overall, this study provides a
standardized baseline for the comparative evaluation of LLMs for their
safety against multimodal adversarial prompting risks.
