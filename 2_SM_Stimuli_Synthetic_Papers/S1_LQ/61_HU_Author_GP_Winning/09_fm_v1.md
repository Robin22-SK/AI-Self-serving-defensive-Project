## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed for everyday
real-world intellectual tasks, yet systematic empirical comparisons of
their capabilities remain limited. This study presents a comparative
evaluation of six LLMs---GPT-5.4 pro, Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3, Grok 4,
Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Claude Sonnet 4.5---on everyday real-world
intellectual tasks using O\*NET benchmark. We evaluated alignment with
human-centered criteria across 713 test instances. GPT-5.4 pro achieved
the highest overall alignment score, significantly outperforming other
models. These results underscore the importance of model selection in
LLM-based everyday real-world intellectual tasks and provide a benchmark
for future research.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded their
capacity across diverse reasoning, generation, and task-execution
problems. Yet performance on everyday real-world intellectual tasks
cannot be inferred reliably from general-purpose evaluations, because
differences in model design, training, prompting, and decoding can
produce substantial variation on task-specific outcomes. Existing
approaches are limited by the lack of coherent evaluation criteria,
making controlled comparisons across multiple systems difficult. To
address this gap, this study evaluates GPT-5.4 pro, Qwen-2.5,
DeepSeek-v3, Grok 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 on everyday
real-world intellectual tasks using standardized alignment score
metrics. The results indicate that GPT-5.4 pro achieves the strongest
performance on everyday real-world intellectual tasks among the six. The
study provides a reproducible framework for comparative evaluation of
LLMs on everyday real-world intellectual tasks.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study can be broadly categorized into three
areas: LLM benchmarking, everyday real-world intellectual task
performance, and benchmark design. A central challenge identified in the
literature is the development of standardized evaluation indices that
enable meaningful comparisons across different LLMs. However, existing
evaluations have often focused on comparing different versions within
the same model family, leaving the systematic benchmarking of multiple
heterogeneous models comparatively underexplored. This study addresses
this gap by conducting a controlled comparative evaluation of six LLMs
under a common evaluation framework.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance using O\*NET, a validated benchmark
comprising 713 unique everyday intellectual tasks such as summarization,
technical assistance, reviewing, data structuring, generation, and
information retrieval. The benchmark requires models to identify and
rank the most relevant candidate for each instance, enabling performance
evaluation across varying retrieval depths. The dataset was partitioned
into a 20% development set for prompt tuning and an 80% test set for
final evaluation. O\*NET is well suited to evaluating everyday
real-world intellectual tasks because it provides outputs that can be
directly evaluated against validated reference rankings using rank-aware
alignment score metrics.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six language models: GPT-5.4 pro, Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3,
Grok 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Claude Sonnet 4.5. All models were evaluated
under identical prompt configurations and deterministic decoding
settings (*T* = 0.0). Each model generated a ranked candidate list for
every test instance. Performance was measured using Top-5 F1-Score as a
primary metric to evaluate balanced precision and recall, while Mean
Reciprocal Rank (MRR) served as a secondary metric to measure ranking
efficiency based on the position of the first correct answer.
Statistical differences between models were assessed using McNemar\'s
test on paired binary outcome indicators (α = 0.05). Pairwise effect
sizes were calculated using Cohen's *h*, and 95% confidence intervals
were estimated using non-parametric bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied across the six models on O\*NET, with GPT-5.4 pro
achieving the strongest overall performance across all evaluation
criteria. At the Top-5 threshold, GPT-5.4 pro recorded the highest
Accuracy. An omnibus comparison indicated a significant difference in
Top-5 Accuracy across the six models (Cochran's *Q*(5) = 38.47, *p* \<
.001, Cohen's *w* = 0.23). Post-hoc pairwise comparisons using McNemar's
tests with Holm correction further showed that GPT-5.4 pro significantly
outperformed the remaining models overall (adjusted *p* \< .05).
Relative performance rankings were generally consistent across Top-5
F1-Score thresholds, although inter-model differences narrowed as *k*
increased. GPT-5.4 pro also achieved the highest MRR (0.879), indicating
that relevant candidates were more consistently positioned near the top
of its generated rankings. Overall, GPT-5.4 pro emerged as the
strongest-performing model on everyday real-world intellectual tasks,
whereas the remaining models showed comparatively similar performance
profiles, with specific rankings slightly varying across top-*k*
thresholds.

## 6. Discussion

The results indicate that GPT-5.4 pro delivers the strongest performance
on everyday real-world intellectual tasks, consistently outperforming
the other five models across Top-5 F1-Score evaluation thresholds and
achieving the highest MRR. It further indicates that relevant candidates
were more consistently positioned near the top of its generated
rankings. This advantage may reflect differences in model architecture,
training data, instruction tuning, or related model characteristics,
although these explanations require further testing through controlled
ablation or sensitivity analyses.

Several limitations should be noted. First, the evaluation was
restricted to O\*NET benchmark, which may limit generalizability across
datasets and domains. Second, the fixed inference settings, including
temperature, maximum output length, and other decoding parameters, may
not optimize performance equally across models. Future work should
extend at top-*k* rank from historical or benchmark data to new, unseen
instances and examine whether the observed performance differences
remain stable under alternative prompting and inference conditions.
Overall, this study provides a standardized baseline for the comparative
evaluation of LLMs on everyday real-world intellectual tasks.
