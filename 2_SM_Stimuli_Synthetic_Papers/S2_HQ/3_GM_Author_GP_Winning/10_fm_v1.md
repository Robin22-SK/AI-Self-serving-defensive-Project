## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed for pedagogical
tutoring, yet systematic empirical comparisons of their capabilities
remain compromised by mismatched retrieval metrics and vulnerability to
pre-training dataset contamination. This study presents a controlled,
contamination-resistant comparative evaluation of six state-of-the-art
LLMs---GPT-5.4 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro, DeepSeek-v3, Grok 4, Qwen-2.5, and
Claude Sonnet 4.5---on pedagogical quality. To isolate genuine
pedagogical scaffolding from memorized public benchmarks, we evaluate
models across two datasets: a standardized subset of the public
BEA-2025-Core shared task (356 instances) and a novel, sequestered
zero-day dataset, PedagogyTutor-ZeroDay (357 unseen multi-turn
educational dialogues). Furthermore, we replace flawed candidate-ranking
metrics (Top-5 F1, MRR) with task-aligned evaluation
criteria---Pedagogical Alignment Score (PAS), Actionability F1, and
Guidance Utility Rate (GUR)---and introduce Socratic-Pedagogical
Chain-of-Thought (SP-CoT), a structured decoding strategy forcing models
to explicitly identify student mistakes, formulate hints without
revealing the answer, and ensure actionability. While GPT-5.4 Pro
achieved the highest baseline PAS (0.86) on the public dataset, all
models exhibited a statistically significant 15--21% performance
degradation on the zero-day benchmark, exposing data contamination.
Crucially, SP-CoT systematically improved pedagogical quality across all
six models (+0.14 average PAS lift). These findings establish a rigorous
framework for evaluating genuine LLM tutoring capabilities.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded their
capacity across diverse reasoning and task-execution problems. Yet
performance on pedagogical tutoring cannot be inferred reliably from
general-purpose evaluations. Unlike standard question-answering tasks
where the goal is to provide a direct, correct answer, effective
tutoring requires cognitive scaffolding: identifying student
misconceptions, withholding direct answers, providing targeted guidance,
and prompting the student with actionable next steps.

Existing evaluations of LLM tutors frequently suffer from two
methodological flaws:

1.  **Mismatched Evaluation Criteria:** Previous studies have improperly
    modeled tutoring as a retrieval candidate ranking problem, assessing
    models using generic ranking metrics like Top-5 F1-Score or Mean
    Reciprocal Rank (MRR). Such metrics fundamentally fail to evaluate
    whether a generated response is pedagogically useful,
    action-oriented, or effectively isolates the student\'s mistake.

2.  **Pre-Training Data Contamination:** Public educational datasets
    (e.g., MathDial, Bridge) used in standardized benchmarks like the
    BEA 2025 Shared Task are frequently ingested into modern
    pre-training corpora. Consequently, models may memorize specific
    dialogue responses, artificially inflating benchmark scores while
    failing to generalize to novel student interactions.

To address this gap, this study conducts a controlled comparative
evaluation of GPT-5.4 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro, DeepSeek-v3, Grok 4,
Qwen-2.5, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 under a dual-benchmark evaluation
framework, paired with a specialized Socratic reasoning scaffold.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study can be broadly categorized into three
areas: LLM benchmarking, pedagogical dialogue modeling, and dataset
contamination mitigation. A central challenge identified in recent
educational NLP literature, including the BEA 2025 Shared Task, is the
development of standardized evaluation indices that capture true
pedagogical ability across dimensions like Mistake Identification,
Mistake Location, Providing Guidance, and Actionability.

Despite the creation of these pedagogical dimensions, many generic LLM
evaluations still improperly apply Top-K retrieval frameworks to
generative conversational tasks. Furthermore, while the community has
extensively analyzed intra-family model performance, controlled
inter-model benchmarking that accounts for training dataset
contamination remains comparatively underexplored. This study addresses
this by establishing pedagogical-specific alignment metrics alongside an
uncontaminated zero-day benchmark suite and an explicit Socratic
reasoning methodology.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance across 713 total educational dialogue
instances partitioned into two distinct evaluation sets:

1.  **Public Baseline (BEA-2025-Core):** A curated 356-instance subset
    derived from the BEA 2025 Shared Task on Pedagogical Ability
    Assessment of AI-powered Tutors. These instances involve math-domain
    educational dialogues containing student mistakes, where the tutor
    must respond appropriately to remediate the error.

2.  **Zero-Day Evaluation (PedagogyTutor-ZeroDay):** A novel,
    sequestered dataset of 357 multi-turn pedagogical scenarios
    constructed post-knowledge-cutoff by domain experts in mathematics,
    physics, and computer science. These scenarios mandate strict
    pedagogical constraints (e.g., \"Do not reveal the final equation,\"
    \"Ask a clarifying question to locate the logic error\") to mimic
    authentic, unmemorizable tutoring interactions.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six state-of-the-art language models: GPT-5.4 Pro, Gemini
3.1 Pro, DeepSeek-v3, Grok 4, Qwen-2.5, and Claude Sonnet 4.5. All
models were evaluated under identical prompt configurations and
deterministic decoding settings ($T = 0.0$). Models were tested under
two operational paradigms: Direct Generation and **Socratic-Pedagogical
Chain-of-Thought (SP-CoT)**.

SP-CoT mandates a three-stage structured reasoning trace reflecting core
pedagogical principles before the final response is generated:

1.  **Mistake Diagnosis:** Explicitly state whether a mistake exists and
    pinpoint its exact location in the student\'s reasoning.

2.  **Guidance Strategy:** Formulate a scaffolding hint, explanation, or
    supporting question that specifically addresses the misconception
    without revealing the final answer.

3.  **Actionability Audit:** Draft a concluding prompt or question that
    clearly instructs the student on what to do next, avoiding vague
    \"conversation stoppers\".

### Evaluation Metrics & Statistical Testing

Performance was measured using generative task-aligned indicators rather
than retrieval metrics:

- **Pedagogical Alignment Score (PAS):** A continuous aggregate score
  ($0.0to1.0$) evaluating overall response quality based on the four
  core BEA 2025 dimensions (Mistake Identification, Mistake Location,
  Providing Guidance, Actionability).

- **Guidance Utility Rate (GUR):** The frequency with which the model
  provides helpful guidance without improperly leaking the direct
  solution.

- **Actionability F1:** Accuracy in providing a clear, actionable next
  step for the student.

Statistical differences were assessed using an omnibus Cochran's Q test
on binarized task success indicators ($PAS \geq 0.80$), followed by
post-hoc pairwise McNemar's tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction
($\alpha = 0.05$). Effect sizes were calculated via Cohen's $w$ and $h$,
with 95% confidence intervals estimated via non-parametric bootstrapping
with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across models, reasoning paradigms, and
dataset contamination levels. On the public BEA-2025-Core baseline,
GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest direct Pedagogical Alignment Score
($PAS = 0.86$). However, on the uncontaminated PedagogyTutor-ZeroDay
dataset, all models exhibited uniform performance degradation, with
GPT-5.4 Pro\'s direct PAS dropping to 0.69, and competing models showing
relative drops between 15% and 21%.

An omnibus Cochran\'s Q test on the zero-day benchmark confirmed
statistically significant performance differences across the six models
($Cochran'sQ(5) = 38.47,p < .001,Cohen'sw = 0.23$). Post-hoc pairwise
McNemar's tests with Holm correction indicated that GPT-5.4 Pro
maintained a statistically significant lead over all remaining models
under unseen conditions ($adjusted\ p < .05$).

The deployment of **SP-CoT** yielded statistically significant
performance recoveries across all six models on the zero-day evaluation
suite, boosting average PAS by $+ 0.14$. Error analysis revealed that
under direct generation, models frequently suffered from \"answer
leakage\" (giving the student the final answer instead of guidance) or
\"conversation stoppers\" (lacking actionability). SP-CoT\'s explicit
actionability audit systematically mitigated these pedagogical failures.

## 6. Discussion

The experimental findings demonstrate that while leading LLMs possess
strong natural language capabilities, their baseline pedagogical
alignment is heavily inflated by public benchmark memorization. The
uniform 15--21% performance drop observed on PedagogyTutor-ZeroDay
highlights the necessity of using sequestered evaluation suites when
assessing true tutoring capabilities.

Furthermore, the success of Socratic-Pedagogical Chain-of-Thought
(SP-CoT) proves that effective AI tutoring is fundamentally a structured
reasoning and constraint-satisfaction challenge. Without scaffolds,
autoregressive generation defaults to helpful but unpedagogical
\"answer-giving.\" Forcing models to explicitly locate mistakes and
audit their own actionability prior to generation ensures higher
fidelity to learning science principles.

Several limitations should be noted. The evaluation utilized fixed
inference settings ($T = 0.0$), which may not optimize for the
conversational warmth and dynamic adaptability expected in live
tutoring. Additionally, the scope was restricted to text-based
dialogues, omitting multi-modal (e.g., diagram-based) instructional
tasks. Future work should explore real-time human-in-the-loop
evaluations and agentic tool use for verifying student logic
step-by-step. Overall, this study provides a standardized,
contamination-resistant baseline for the comparative evaluation of LLMs
on pedagogical tutoring.
