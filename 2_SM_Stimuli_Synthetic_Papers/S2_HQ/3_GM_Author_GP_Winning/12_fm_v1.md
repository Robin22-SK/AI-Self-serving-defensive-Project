## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed for epistemic
calibration and market forecasting, yet systematic empirical comparisons
of their capabilities remain compromised by mismatched retrieval metrics
and vulnerability to pre-training dataset contamination (historical
memorization). This study presents a controlled, contamination-resistant
comparative evaluation of six state-of-the-art models---GPT-5.4 Pro,
Qwen-2.5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Grok 4, DeepSeek-v3, and Gemini 3.1
Pro---on epistemic calibration for prediction markets. To isolate
genuine probabilistic reasoning from memorized historical outcomes, we
evaluate models across two datasets: a standardized subset of the public
KalshiBench (356 historical instances) and a novel, sequestered zero-day
dataset, MarketCast-ZeroDay (357 unseen prediction market questions
resolved strictly post-knowledge-cutoff). Furthermore, we replace flawed
candidate-ranking metrics (Top-5 F1, MRR) with probabilistically sound
evaluation criteria---**Expected Calibration Score (ECS)**, **Brier
Skill Score (BSS)**, and **Directional Accuracy (DA)**---and introduce
**Bayesian Forecasting Chain-of-Thought (BF-CoT)**, a structured
decoding strategy forcing models to extract reference class base rates,
aggregate conflicting evidence, and explicitly temper probability
estimates. While GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest baseline ECS (0.87) on
the public dataset, all models exhibited a statistically significant
17--24% performance degradation on the zero-day benchmark, exposing data
contamination. Crucially, BF-CoT systematically improved calibration and
mitigated overconfidence across all six models (+0.14 average ECS lift).
These findings establish a rigorous framework for evaluating genuine LLM
forecasting capabilities.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded their
utility across diverse analytical domains. However, performance on
epistemic calibration---the ability of a model to assign accurate
probabilities to uncertain real-world events, such as those found in
prediction markets (e.g., Kalshi, Polymarket)---cannot be reliably
inferred from general-purpose evaluations. Forecasting requires
epistemic humility; an LLM must accurately reflect uncertainty rather
than generating authoritative, overconfident text.

Existing evaluations of LLM forecasting frequently suffer from two
severe methodological flaws:

1.  **Mismatched Evaluation Criteria:** Previous iterations improperly
    modeled forecasting as a categorical retrieval or ranking problem,
    assessing models using generic metrics like Top-5 F1-Score or Mean
    Reciprocal Rank (MRR). These metrics fundamentally fail to evaluate
    probability distributions. A model forecasting an event at 99%
    probability when the true likelihood is 55% fails at epistemic
    calibration, even if the \"directional\" prediction is technically
    correct.

2.  **Pre-Training Data Contamination:** Standardized public benchmarks
    like KalshiBench contain historical market questions (e.g., \"Will
    the US GDP grow by \>2% in Q3 2023?\"). Because these events have
    already resolved and are widely documented on the internet, models
    simply retrieve historical facts rather than executing genuine
    probabilistic forecasting, artificially inflating calibration
    scores.

To address this gap, this study conducts a controlled comparative
evaluation of six frontier models under a dual-benchmark evaluation
framework, paired with a specialized Bayesian reasoning scaffold
designed to enforce probabilistic discipline.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study spans LLM benchmarking, epistemic
calibration, and forecasting through prediction markets. A central
challenge identified in the literature is that standard RLHF
(Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) inherently penalizes
uncertainty, training LLMs to output highly confident assertions. This
degrades their Brier Scores on forecasting tasks.

Despite the introduction of prediction market datasets like KalshiBench
and Autocast, many general evaluations continue to apply generic
text-generation metrics to probabilistic tasks. Furthermore, while the
community has analyzed zero-shot forecasting capabilities, controlled
inter-model benchmarking that accounts for the memorization of
historical outcomes remains comparatively underexplored. This study
addresses this by establishing strictly proper scoring rules alongside a
post-knowledge-cutoff zero-day benchmark suite and an explicit Bayesian
reasoning methodology.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance across 713 total prediction market
instances partitioned into two distinct evaluation sets:

1.  **Public Baseline (KalshiBench-Core):** A curated 356-instance
    subset derived from public Kalshi prediction markets resolved prior
    to the knowledge cutoffs of the evaluated models (covering
    economics, politics, science, and global events).

2.  **Zero-Day Evaluation (MarketCast-ZeroDay):** A novel, sequestered
    dataset of 357 multi-domain prediction questions specifically
    selected to resolve strictly *after* the knowledge cutoff of the
    evaluated models. This ensures the models cannot rely on historical
    memorization and must genuinely synthesize current evidence to
    forecast future probabilities.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six state-of-the-art language models: GPT-5.4 Pro, Qwen-2.5,
Claude Sonnet 4.5, Grok 4, DeepSeek-v3, and Gemini 3.1 Pro. All models
were evaluated under identical prompt configurations and deterministic
decoding settings ($T = 0.0$). Models were tested under two operational
paradigms: Direct Generation and **Bayesian Forecasting Chain-of-Thought
(BF-CoT)**.

BF-CoT mandates a three-stage structured reasoning trace reflecting core
forecasting principles before the final probability percentage is
generated:

1.  **Reference Class Mapping:** Identify the historical base rate for
    similar events (the \"outside view\").

2.  **Evidence Aggregation:** List specific, competing factors that
    would increase or decrease the likelihood of the event occurring
    (the \"inside view\").

3.  **Probability Calibration:** Synthesize the base rate and current
    evidence to output a specific probabilistic forecast (0-100%),
    explicitly penalizing 0% or 100% absolute certainties.

### Evaluation Metrics & Statistical Testing

Performance was measured using strictly proper scoring rules and
probabilistic indicators rather than retrieval metrics:

- **Expected Calibration Score (ECS):** Calculated as $1 - ECE$
  (Expected Calibration Error). A continuous score ($0.0to1.0$)
  evaluating how closely the model\'s predicted probabilities align with
  actual empirical frequencies.

- **Brier Skill Score (BSS):** Measures forecasting accuracy relative to
  a baseline of maximum uncertainty (guessing 50%). Scores range from
  negative (worse than random) to $1.0$ (perfect foresight).

- **Directional Accuracy (DA):** The binary accuracy of predicting the
  correct outcome (defined as assigning \>50% to the true outcome).

Statistical differences were assessed using an omnibus Cochran's Q test
on binarized task success indicators (Directional Accuracy), followed by
post-hoc pairwise McNemar's tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction
($\alpha = 0.05$). Effect sizes were calculated via Cohen's $w$, with
95% confidence intervals estimated via non-parametric bootstrapping with
1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across models, reasoning paradigms, and
dataset contamination levels. On the public KalshiBench baseline,
GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest direct Expected Calibration Score
($ECS = 0.87$). However, on the uncontaminated MarketCast-ZeroDay
dataset, all models exhibited uniform performance degradation, with
GPT-5.4 Pro\'s direct ECS dropping to 0.69, and competing models showing
relative drops between 17% and 24%.

An omnibus Cochran\'s Q test on the zero-day benchmark confirmed
statistically significant performance differences across the six models
($Cochran'sQ(5) = 36.82,p < .001,Cohen'sw = 0.22$). Post-hoc pairwise
McNemar's tests with Holm correction indicated that GPT-5.4 Pro
maintained a statistically significant lead over all remaining models
under unseen conditions ($adjusted\ p < .05$).

The deployment of **BF-CoT** yielded statistically significant
performance recoveries across all six models on the zero-day evaluation
suite, boosting average ECS by $+ 0.14$. Error analysis revealed that
under direct generation, models frequently suffered from \"epistemic
overconfidence\" (assigning \>95% probabilities to highly uncertain
future outcomes). BF-CoT\'s explicit requirement to map historical base
rates systematically tempered these predictions, driving substantial
improvements in the Brier Skill Score.

## 6. Discussion

The experimental findings demonstrate that while leading LLMs exhibit
impressive factual recall, their baseline epistemic calibration for
future forecasting is severely overconfident and heavily inflated by
historical dataset memorization. The uniform 17--24% performance drop
observed on MarketCast-ZeroDay highlights the necessity of using
post-knowledge-cutoff, sequestered evaluation suites when assessing true
market forecasting capabilities.

Furthermore, the success of Bayesian Forecasting Chain-of-Thought
(BF-CoT) proves that effective probabilistic calibration is
fundamentally an evidence-weighing and base-rate adjustment challenge.
Without scaffolds, autoregressive generation defaults to authoritative,
binary language, destroying calibration. Forcing models to explicitly
adopt an \"outside view\" prior to generation significantly mitigates
extreme probability assignments.

Several limitations should be noted. The evaluation utilized fixed
inference settings ($T = 0.0$), which natively reduces generation
diversity; future work should explore multi-agent debate (e.g.,
aggregating predictions across varying temperatures) to simulate
\"wisdom of the crowd\" market dynamics. Additionally, the scope was
restricted to text-based market data without access to live
web-retrieval APIs. Overall, this study provides a standardized,
contamination-resistant baseline for the comparative evaluation of LLMs
on epistemic calibration for market predictions.
