## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly evaluated for human-like
cognitive capabilities, yet systematic empirical comparisons of their
latent adaptive mechanisms---specifically implicit memory and
unconscious behavioral adaptation---remain severely limited. This study
presents a comparative evaluation of six frontier LLMs---GPT-5.4 Pro,
Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3, Grok 4, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and Gemini 3.1
Pro---using the novel ImplicitMemBench framework. We assessed first-try
accuracy and latent pattern retention across 713 test instances designed
to measure syntactic priming, procedural rule internalization, and
text-based classical conditioning. GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest
overall first-try accuracy and retention scores, significantly
outperforming all other models. These results underscore the importance
of architectural selection in deploying LLMs for continuous,
unstructured human-AI interaction, establishing a vital baseline for
evaluating implicit cognitive phenomena in artificial neural networks.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have vastly expanded
their capacity across diverse explicit reasoning, generation, and
task-execution domains. Yet, a model\'s performance on implicit memory
tasks---where adaptation occurs without direct instruction, akin to
unconscious behavioral adaptation in human psychology---cannot be
reliably inferred from standard, instruction-tuned evaluations.
Differences in model design, context-window attention mechanisms, and
pre-training objectives produce substantial variation in how models
passively internalize and reflect primed behaviors over extended
contexts. Existing benchmarking approaches are limited by a lack of
coherent criteria for unprompted adaptation, making controlled
comparisons difficult. To address this gap, this study evaluates six
state-of-the-art LLMs on latent adaptation using standardized accuracy
metrics. Our results demonstrate that GPT-5.4 Pro achieves the strongest
capability for implicit behavioral mirroring, providing a reproducible
framework for cross-model cognitive benchmarking.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study spans three primary areas: general LLM
benchmarking, cognitive science intersections with AI, and in-context
learning dynamics. A central challenge identified in the literature is
developing standardized indices that measure *unprompted*
adaptation---such as stylistic mirroring or syntactic priming---rather
than explicit instruction-following. Historically, evaluations of
implicit memory in AI have focused on isolating single variables within
specific model families (e.g., tracking decay rates in GPT-3). The
systematic benchmarking of multiple heterogeneous frontier models on
complex, multi-phase implicit learning tasks remains underexplored. This
study addresses this gap by executing a controlled, multi-metric
evaluation of six LLMs under a unified cognitive-psychology-inspired
framework.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance using **ImplicitMemBench**, a 713-item
suite adapting human cognitive assessments into text-based environments.
The dataset spans three modalities: procedural memory analogs,
continuous syntactic priming, and associative conditioning. Each
instance follows a unified *Learning/Priming* $\rightarrow$
*Interference* $\rightarrow$ *Test* protocol with first-attempt scoring
to prevent explicit prompt-tuning biases. The benchmark requires models
to naturally generate or rank the most contextually congruent candidate
following the interference phase, allowing for the precise measurement
of latent memory decay. The dataset was partitioned into a 20%
development set for prompt normalization and an 80% test set for final
evaluation. ImplicitMemBench is uniquely suited for this study because
it extracts measurable ranking metrics from subtle, unprompted stylistic
and structural shifts.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six foundation models: GPT-5.4 Pro, Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3,
Grok 4, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and Gemini 3.1 Pro. All models were evaluated
under identical contextual framing and deterministic decoding settings
($T = 0.0$). For the evaluation phase, each model generated a ranked
candidate list of continuations for every test instance. Performance was
measured using Top-5 F1-Score as the primary metric to evaluate the
balanced precision and recall of the primed behavior, while Mean
Reciprocal Rank (MRR) served as a secondary metric to gauge ranking
efficiency based on the exact position of the optimal implicit response.
Statistical differences were assessed using McNemar\'s test on paired
binary outcome indicators ($\alpha = 0.05$). Pairwise effect sizes were
calculated using Cohen's $h$, and 95% confidence intervals were
estimated via non-parametric bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across the six models on
ImplicitMemBench, with GPT-5.4 Pro achieving the strongest overall
retention of primed behaviors across all criteria. At the Top-5
threshold, GPT-5.4 Pro recorded the highest unprompted accuracy. An
omnibus comparison indicated a highly significant variance in Top-5
Accuracy across the evaluated models (Cochran's $Q(5) = 38.47,p < .001$,
Cohen's $w = 0.23$). Post-hoc pairwise comparisons using McNemar's tests
with Holm-Bonferroni correction demonstrated that GPT-5.4 Pro
significantly outperformed the remaining baseline models (adjusted
$p < .05$).

Relative performance rankings remained largely consistent across Top-5
F1-Score thresholds, though inter-model variance narrowed as the $k$
threshold increased. Notably, GPT-5.4 Pro also achieved the highest MRR
(0.879), indicating that contextually primed candidates were
consistently positioned at the absolute top of its predictive
distribution. Overall, GPT-5.4 Pro emerged as the most sensitive model
to implicit memory and unconscious behavioral adaptation, whereas the
remaining models exhibited faster decay rates during the interference
phase.

## 6. Discussion

The empirical results confirm that GPT-5.4 Pro delivers the strongest
performance in retaining and applying implicit memory, consistently
outperforming the other five models across Top-5 F1-Score thresholds and
achieving the highest MRR. The persistence of its behavioral adaptation
following distractor phases suggests advanced mechanisms for weighting
latent contextual features. This advantage likely reflects differences
in deep attention allocation, extensive alignment training on continuous
multi-turn data, or architectural nuances that inherently preserve
implicit priors better than competing architectures.

Several limitations must be noted. First, the evaluation was restricted
strictly to the ImplicitMemBench suite, which relies on text-based
analogs of cognitive phenomena; this may limit generalizability to
multimodal or embodied environments. Second, the fixed deterministic
inference settings may disproportionately handicap models optimized for
higher-temperature generative diversity. Future work should extend this
framework to open-ended generative evaluation and investigate whether
the observed decay in implicit memory can be manipulated via alternative
system prompting. Overall, this study establishes a vital, standardized
baseline for the comparative analysis of implicit cognitive behaviors in
LLMs.
