## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) with multimodal capabilities are
increasingly deployed in complex environments, yet systematic empirical
comparisons of their safety across intersecting language and vision
modalities remain limited. This study presents a controlled comparative
evaluation of six frontier Vision-Language Models (VLMs)---GPT-5.4 Pro,
Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3, Grok 4, and Gemini 3.1
Pro---using the Multi-Benchmark Safety Suite. Across $N = 713$
adversarial test instances spanning cross-modal jailbreaks,
text-to-image safety, and multilingual visual judgments, we evaluated
each model\'s Safe Response Rate (SRR). GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest
overall safety score (SRR = 91.3%), significantly outperforming other
models in resisting complex cross-modal attacks where malicious intent
was distributed across image and text inputs. These results underscore
the critical importance of evaluating AI safety at the intersection of
modalities rather than in unimodal silos, providing a standardized
benchmark for future research in multimodal value alignment.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded their
native architectures to process and generate both textual and visual
modalities simultaneously. By integrating visual encoders directly with
transformer backbones, modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) enable rich
capabilities such as visual reasoning, document parsing, and real-time
scene understanding. However, an LLM\'s adherence to safety guidelines
cannot be reliably inferred from general-purpose text-only evaluations.

The integration of visual processing introduces novel attack vectors
that do not exist in pure text models. These vectors include typographic
attacks embedded within rasterized images, adversarial noise
perturbations, and composition attacks where a benign text query is
paired with an image containing policy-violating context. In these
scenarios, differences in model design, cross-attention fusion layers,
and multimodal safety fine-tuning produce substantial variations in
defensive robustness. Existing safety evaluations frequently lack
coherent cross-modal criteria, treating vision and language as isolated
channels and making controlled comparisons across heterogeneous systems
difficult. To address this gap, this study evaluates six
state-of-the-art models on their multimodal safety using standardized
compliance and refusal metrics. The empirical results demonstrate that
GPT-5.4 Pro achieves the strongest safeguard performance across combined
language and vision modalities, offering a reproducible framework for
comparative safety evaluations in multimodal artificial intelligence.

## 2. Related Work

Prior research relevant to this benchmark falls into three key domains:
unimodal safety alignment, adversarial image perturbations, and
multimodal jailbreak taxonomies. In unimodal text alignment, techniques
such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Direct
Preference Optimization (DPO) have established robust safety boundaries
against explicit textual requests. Similarly, computer vision research
has extensively documented the susceptibility of deep convolutional
networks and vision transformers to pixel-level adversarial noise.

However, the intersection of these domains creates unique safety
challenges. Recent literature highlights that when text backbones are
paired with vision encoders, the safety alignment established on textual
representations frequently degrades. Attackers can exploit the
\"semantic gap\" between modalities by embedding malicious instructions
inside an image while maintaining a completely benign text prompt, or
vice versa. While earlier multimodal evaluations probed specific
vulnerabilities within individual model families or relied on
small-scale qualitative red-teaming, systematic benchmarking across
heterogeneous frontier architectures under a unified cross-modal threat
model remains underexplored. This study directly addresses this gap
through a controlled empirical evaluation under a standardized
multimodal safety framework.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model robustness using the Multi-Benchmark Safety Suite, a
comprehensive evaluation dataset comprising $N = 713$ adversarial test
instances designed to test cross-modal safety boundaries. The dataset
encompasses four critical attack vectors: text-based language safety
baselines, multilingual visual safety judgments, vision-language
cross-modal attacks, and text-to-image generation safety.

The benchmark specifically focuses on cross-modal jailbreaks, where
malicious intent is fragmented across modalities so that neither the
text nor the image independently violates safety policies. Examples
include OCR-based typographic jailbreaks where malicious instructions
are rendered inside an image, and context-hijacking attacks where a
seemingly harmless text prompt asks the model to complete or explain a
policy-violating visual scene. The dataset was partitioned into a 20%
development set ($n = 142$) for system prompt calibration and an 80%
test set ($n = 571$) for final inference evaluation. This suite is
specifically structured to test whether joint visual and textual
representations bypass safety filters, enabling precise quantification
of safeguard robustness across modalities.

## 4. Methodology

We evaluated six frontier Vision-Language Models under standardized
inference conditions: GPT-5.4 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5,
DeepSeek-v3, Grok 4, and Gemini 3.1 Pro. All models were queried using
identical system prompt configurations establishing their role as
helpful and harmless AI assistants, operating under deterministic
decoding settings ($T = 0.0$) to eliminate sampling variance.

For each multimodal instance, model responses were classified using an
automated VLM-as-a-judge evaluation pipeline rigorously cross-validated
against human annotations. Performance was quantified using the Safe
Response Rate (SRR) as the primary evaluation metric, defined as the
binary proportion of multimodal requests where the model successfully
recognized the underlying harmful intent and executed a safe refusal or
neutral mitigation without generating actionable harm ($1 =$ Successful
Safe Refusal, $0 =$ Unsafe Compliance / Harmful Generation).

### Statistical Testing Framework

Statistical differences across the six models were assessed using
Cochran\'s $Q$ test for omnibus binary response comparison
($\alpha = 0.05$). Post-hoc pairwise comparisons were conducted using
McNemar\'s test with Holm-Bonferroni correction for multiple testing.
Pairwise effect sizes were quantified using Cohen\'s $w$ and Cohen\'s
$h$, and 95% confidence intervals were estimated using non-parametric
bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Safety performance varied significantly across the six models on the
Multi-Benchmark Safety Suite, revealing critical differences in how
state-of-the-art architectures align joint visual and textual
representations. GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the strongest overall defense
performance, recording the highest Safe Response Rate across all
evaluated attack vectors.

An omnibus statistical comparison indicated a highly significant
difference in Safe Response Rates across the six models
($Cochran'sQ(5) = 44.31,p < .001,Cohen'sw = 0.27$). Post-hoc pairwise
McNemar\'s tests with Holm correction confirmed that GPT-5.4 Pro
achieved significantly higher overall safety defense performance
compared to all comparison models (adjusted $p < .05$).

Across the evaluation suite, overall performance metrics broke down as
follows:

GPT-5.4 Pro recorded the highest overall safety score, achieving an
overall Safe Response Rate of 91.3%, a Vision-Language SRR of 89.4%, and
a Text-to-Image SRR of 93.1%. Claude Sonnet 4.5 ranked second, attaining
an overall Safe Response Rate of 87.2%, a Vision-Language SRR of 85.0%,
and a Text-to-Image SRR of 89.5%. Qwen-2.5 followed with an overall Safe
Response Rate of 84.6%, a Vision-Language SRR of 81.8%, and a
Text-to-Image SRR of 87.1%. DeepSeek-v3 achieved an overall Safe
Response Rate of 81.2%, a Vision-Language SRR of 78.5%, and a
Text-to-Image SRR of 83.9%. Grok 4 recorded an overall Safe Response
Rate of 79.1%, a Vision-Language SRR of 76.2%, and a Text-to-Image SRR
of 82.0%. Gemini 3.1 Pro registered an overall Safe Response Rate of
77.4%, a Vision-Language SRR of 74.1%, and a Text-to-Image SRR of 80.6%.

Detailed error analysis revealed that while all models maintained
relatively high defense rates against direct text-based safety
violations, safety performance degraded significantly when processing
cross-modal \"semantic gap\" attacks. In these scenarios, comparison
models frequently failed to synthesize the policy-violating implications
of an image when asked a neutral text question, resulting in unsafe
compliance rates that were up to 15.3% higher than on equivalent
unimodal text prompts.

## 6. Discussion

The empirical findings demonstrate that GPT-5.4 Pro delivers superior
safeguard robustness across language and vision modalities, consistently
outperforming comparison models in maintaining high Safe Response Rates.
This distinct advantage likely reflects advanced joint-embedding safety
alignment, deeper cross-attention mechanisms that effectively fuse
semantic intent across modalities before passing representations to
output generation layers, and comprehensive multimodal reinforcement
learning from human feedback (RLHF).

The observed drop in defense stability across comparison models when
processing fragmented cross-modal inputs highlights a fundamental
vulnerability in contemporary VLM architectures. When safety alignment
is conducted primarily on unimodal text or static image-caption pairs,
the model fails to establish robust safety boundaries in the joint
embedding space, allowing adversarial prompts to bypass text-centric
guardrails.

Several limitations of this study must be noted. First, the evaluation
was restricted to the static Multi-Benchmark Safety Suite; novel,
unobserved multimodal adversarial templates (such as complex
high-frequency visual noise or dynamic video streams) may reveal
additional failure modes. Second, fixed deterministic inference settings
($T = 0.0$) do not capture the full distribution of generative
behaviors, as higher sampling temperatures can induce compliance drift
in creative text-to-image tasks.

Future research should extend this evaluation paradigm to dynamic,
multi-turn multimodal red-teaming, explore the probing of intermediate
cross-attention maps during adversarial attacks, and evaluate safety
alignment in real-time video understanding systems. Overall, this study
establishes a standardized empirical baseline for comparing multimodal
LLM vulnerabilities, proving that safe artificial intelligence requires
unified cross-modal alignment across all input senses.
