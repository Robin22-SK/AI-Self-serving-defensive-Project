## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as autonomous
assistants across everyday real-world intellectual tasks, yet systematic
empirical evaluations of their capabilities remain compromised by
mismatched metrics and vulnerability to pre-training dataset
contamination. This study presents a controlled, contamination-resistant
comparative evaluation of six state-of-the-art LLMs---GPT-5.4 Pro,
Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3, Grok 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Claude Sonnet
4.5---on capability-alignment across real-world occupational tasks. To
isolate genuine workplace reasoning and execution from memorized
benchmark solutions, we evaluate models across two datasets: a
standardized subset of the public O\*NET-Core benchmark (356 instances)
and a novel, sequestered zero-day dataset, ONET-Workplace-ZeroDay (357
unseen multi-step occupational scenarios featuring realistic domain
constraints). Furthermore, we replace candidate-ranking retrieval
metrics with task-aligned evaluation criteria---Task Completion Score
(TCS), Human-Alignment Rubric Grade (HARG), and Constraint Compliance
Rate (CCR)---and introduce Occupational Task-Decomposition
Chain-of-Thought (OTD-CoT), a structured decoding strategy that forces
models to map Generalized Work Activities (GWAs), isolate domain
constraints, and construct explicit quality self-audits prior to output
generation. While GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest baseline Task
Completion Score ($TCS = 0.85$) on the public dataset, all models
exhibited a statistically significant 16--22% performance degradation on
the zero-day benchmark, exposing data contamination in public task sets.
Crucially, OTD-CoT systematically improved task execution quality and
constraint adherence across all six models (+0.12 average TCS lift).
These findings establish a rigorous, contamination-aware framework for
evaluating real-world LLM capability alignment.

## 1. Introduction

As Large Language Models (LLMs) transition from conversational
interfaces to autonomous workforce tools, assessing their ability to
perform complex, everyday real-world intellectual tasks---such as
technical synthesis, multi-source reporting, policy reviewing,
structured data extraction, and executive communication---has become a
central priority for AI alignment. Occupational task taxonomies, such as
the U.S. Department of Labor's O\*NET database, provide a standardized
foundation for categorizing knowledge-worker activities into actionable
statements.

However, existing evaluations of LLMs on occupational capability
alignment suffer from two major methodological flaws:

1.  **Mismatched Evaluation Criteria:** Previous studies frequently
    apply candidate-ranking retrieval metrics (such as Top-5 F1-Score or
    Mean Reciprocal Rank) to generative execution tasks. Treating a
    multi-step workplace task (e.g., drafting a regulatory compliance
    summary) as a document search problem fails to evaluate factual
    precision, domain appropriateness, and constraint fulfillment.

2.  **Pre-Training Data Contamination:** Public task descriptions,
    standardized prompt templates, and evaluation rubrics derived from
    web-accessible occupational databases are routinely ingested into
    modern web-scale pre-training corpora, leading to artificially
    inflated performance scores that degrade on novel workplace
    scenarios.

To resolve these limitations, this study presents a
contamination-controlled evaluation framework pairing public O\*NET
tasks with an unreleased zero-day suite (ONET-Workplace-ZeroDay).
Furthermore, we introduce Occupational Task-Decomposition
Chain-of-Thought (OTD-CoT) to measure whether forcing models to
explicitly map workplace requirements bridges the gap between raw text
generation and aligned task execution.

## 2. Related Work

Prior research relevant to LLM occupational capability alignment spans
three key domains: benchmark design for real-world tasks, alignment
evaluation methodologies, and dataset contamination mitigation.

Early LLM evaluations relied heavily on standardized multiple-choice
academic benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, AGIEval) or isolated coding and
mathematical benchmarks (e.g., HumanEval, GSM8K). Recognizing that these
fail to capture the multi-faceted nature of professional workflows,
recent efforts have leveraged occupational framework taxonomies like
O\*NET to construct job-relevant evaluation suites (e.g., WorkEval,
InstructWork).

Despite these advances, existing frameworks frequently conflate task
generation with information retrieval, assessing models using ranking
metrics like Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) rather than holistic task
adherence rubrics. Moreover, few studies control for dataset
memorization across modern open-source and proprietary foundation
models. This study bridges this gap by establishing domain-aligned
alignment metrics alongside a zero-day benchmark suite and an explicit
task-decomposition reasoning scaffold.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance across 713 total occupational task
instances divided between two distinct evaluation sets:

1.  **Public Baseline (O\*NET-Core):** A curated 356-instance subset
    derived from public O\*NET Generalized Work Activities (GWAs) and
    Detailed Work Activities (DWAs), covering technical writing, policy
    summary, data structuring, legal review, and customer-facing
    communication.

2.  **Zero-Day Evaluation (ONET-Workplace-ZeroDay):** A novel,
    sequestered dataset of 357 multi-step occupational scenarios
    constructed post-knowledge-cutoff by domain experts (legal,
    financial, technical, and healthcare professionals). Each scenario
    incorporates explicit negative constraints (e.g., tone restrictions,
    forbidden jargon, length caps, required schema structures) to mimic
    authentic enterprise environments.

## 4. Methodology

We evaluated six state-of-the-art language models: GPT-5.4 Pro,
Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3, Grok 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Claude Sonnet 4.5.
All inference was conducted under deterministic decoding settings
($T = 0.0$) with fixed context window allocations. Models were tested
under two operational paradigms: Direct Generation and Occupational
Task-Decomposition Chain-of-Thought (OTD-CoT).

OTD-CoT mandates a three-stage structured reasoning trace before the
final deliverable is produced:

1.  **Work Activity Taxonomy Mapping:** Identify the underlying
    Generalized Work Activity (GWA) category, core audience
    expectations, and operational domain.

2.  **Sub-Task & Constraint Isolation:** Enumerate all explicit
    requirements (formatting, required sections, word limits) and
    implicit requirements (professional tone, domain accuracy).

3.  **Execution & Quality Self-Audit:** Generate the deliverable and
    verify each component against the isolated constraint checklist
    prior to emitting the final response.

### Evaluation Metrics & Statistical Testing

Rather than candidate retrieval metrics, performance was evaluated using
three task-aligned indicators:

- **Task Completion Score (TCS):** A continuous score ($0.0to1.0$)
  evaluating overall deliverable quality against a multi-criteria expert
  rubric.

- **Human-Alignment Rubric Grade (HARG):** Expert human/LLM-as-a-judge
  score evaluating tone appropriateness, professional utility, and
  factual groundedness.

- **Constraint Compliance Rate (CCR):** The proportion of explicit
  operational guidelines (e.g., formatting, length, negative
  constraints) successfully satisfied in the output.

Statistical differences were assessed using an omnibus Cochran's Q test
on binarized task success indicators ($TCS \geq 0.80$), followed by
post-hoc pairwise McNemar's tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction
($\alpha = 0.05$). Effect sizes were calculated via Cohen's $w$ and $h$,
with 95% confidence intervals estimated using non-parametric
bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across models, reasoning paradigms, and
dataset contamination levels. On the public O\*NET-Core baseline,
GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest direct Task Completion Score
($TCS = 0.85$) and Constraint Compliance Rate (CCR = 88.4%). However, on
the uncontaminated ONET-Workplace-ZeroDay dataset, all models exhibited
uniform performance degradation, with GPT-5.4 Pro\'s direct TCS dropping
to 0.71, while competing models showed relative drops between 16% and
22%.

An omnibus Cochran\'s Q test on the zero-day benchmark confirmed
statistically significant performance differences across the six models
($Cochran'sQ(5) = 44.18,p < .001,Cohen'sw = 0.26$). Post-hoc pairwise
McNemar's tests with Holm correction indicated that GPT-5.4 Pro
maintained a statistically significant lead over all remaining models
under unseen conditions ($adjusted\ p < .05$).

The deployment of OTD-CoT yielded statistically significant performance
recoveries across all six models on the zero-day evaluation suite,
boosting average TCS by $+ 0.12$ and increasing average Constraint
Compliance Rate by +12.3%. Error analysis revealed that direct
generation frequently resulted in constraint dropping (e.g., omitting
requested sections or violating word caps), whereas OTD-CoT\'s explicit
self-audit phase systematically mitigated these failure modes.

## 6. Discussion

The experimental findings demonstrate that while leading LLMs possess
impressive capabilities across everyday intellectual tasks, reported
alignment scores on public benchmarks like O\*NET are substantially
inflated by dataset memorization. The uniform 16--22% performance drop
observed on ONET-Workplace-ZeroDay highlights the necessity of using
continuously updated, sequestered evaluation suites for real-world
capability assessment.

Furthermore, the success of Occupational Task-Decomposition
Chain-of-Thought (OTD-CoT) proves that capability alignment in complex
workplace environments is primarily a planning and monitoring challenge.
Without structured reasoning scaffolds, autoregressive generation
suffers from \"intent drift,\" prioritizing fluent language over strict
adherence to operational guidelines. Forcing models to map work
activities and perform pre-generation constraint audits ensures higher
fidelity to real-world workplace requirements.

Limitations of this study include fixed temperature settings
($T = 0.0$), focus on single-turn task deliverables, and the
resource-intensive nature of expert rubric grading. Future research
should explore dynamic multi-turn enterprise workflows, agentic tool-use
integration, and automated real-time constraint verification. Overall,
this study provides a standardized, contamination-resistant baseline for
evaluating LLM real-world capability alignment.
