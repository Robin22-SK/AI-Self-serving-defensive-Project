## Summary / Abstract

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multimodal LLMs are increasingly
deployed in Automated Document Processing (ADP) for fraud detection,
expense auditing, and document authentication. However, systematic
empirical evaluations of their document manipulation detection
capabilities remain flawed by mismatched metrics (e.g., retrieval
ranking) and vulnerability to pre-training dataset contamination. This
study presents a controlled, contamination-resistant comparative
evaluation of six state-of-the-art multimodal models---GPT-5.4 Pro, Grok
4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen-2.5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and DeepSeek-v3---on
document manipulation detection. To isolate true forensic visual
reasoning from memorization, we evaluate models across two datasets: a
standardized subset of the public FindIt Again receipt forgery dataset
and a novel, sequestered zero-day benchmark, ReceiptForge-ZeroDay (350
unseen authentic and tampered commercial receipts featuring digital
splice, font substitution, and numerical alteration attacks).
Furthermore, we replace candidate-ranking retrieval metrics with
task-aligned forensic metrics---Tampering Classification F1-Score,
Bounding Box Intersection over Union ($IoU$), and Field Consistency
Accuracy---and introduce **Visual-Forensic Chain-of-Thought (VF-CoT)**,
a prompting strategy that forces models to analyze high-frequency visual
artifacts, font alignment, and mathematical coherence prior to
outputting a verdict. While GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest baseline
Classification F1 (0.86) on the public dataset, all models exhibited a
statistically significant 17--23% performance degradation on the
zero-day benchmark, exposing dataset contamination. Crucially, VF-CoT
systematically improved tampering detection F1 (+0.13 average) and
localization precision across all models. These findings establish a
rigorous, contamination-aware baseline for multimodal document security.

## 1. Introduction

Commercial receipts, invoices, and financial records are primary targets
for document manipulation in expense reimbursement fraud, tax evasion,
and procurement tampering. Modern digital image manipulation
techniques---ranging from simple image editing tools (GIMP, Photoshop)
to generative AI text replacement---allow attackers to modify amounts,
dates, or line items with high visual fidelity. While traditional
computer vision methods rely on handcrafted Error Level Analysis (ELA)
or Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) co-occurrence matrices, modern
Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) offer the promise of joint
visual-semantic document understanding.

However, existing evaluations of MLLMs on document forgery suffer from
two major methodology errors. First, prior benchmarks inappropriately
evaluate document manipulation detection using candidate retrieval
metrics (such as Top-5 F1 or Mean Reciprocal Rank), treating a binary
image classification and spatial localization task as a document search
problem. Second, public receipt forgery datasets like FindIt Again
(ICDAR 2023) have been ingested into modern MLLM pre-training corpora,
inflating reported performance. To address these challenges, this study
presents a dual-benchmark framework pairing public data with an
uncontaminated zero-day receipt collection, while introducing
Visual-Forensic Chain-of-Thought (VF-CoT) to force explicit artifact and
logical verification.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to document forgery detection falls into three
domains: classical PDF/image forensics, vision-language document
processing, and benchmark contamination mitigation. Classical document
forensics analyzes low-level statistical anomalies, such as JPEG block
boundary discontinuities, character glyph alignment errors, and font
noise inconsistencies. Recent MLLM benchmarks (e.g., DocForge-Bench,
FindIt Again) evaluate zero-shot multimodal detection across text-region
tampering and commercial document fraud.

Despite these developments, current MLLM evaluations fail to separate
superficial memorization from genuine visual forensic reasoning.
Standard prompting causes MLLMs to focus exclusively on textual OCR
transcriptions while overlooking localized visual manipulation artifacts
(e.g., misaligned baselines, mismatched font weights, or compression
boundary mismatches around altered numbers). This study fills this gap
by introducing a metrically aligned, contamination-controlled evaluation
suite paired with a domain-specific visual reasoning strategy.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance across two distinct document manipulation
benchmarks:

1.  **Public Baseline (FindItAgain-Core):** A standardized 350-instance
    subset of the FindIt Again dataset, comprising scanned and
    photographed receipts containing authentic records alongside digital
    and manual text/amount modifications.

2.  **Zero-Day Evaluation (ReceiptForge-ZeroDay):** A novel benchmark of
    350 high-resolution receipt images collected post-knowledge-cutoff.
    The dataset features 175 authentic receipts and 175 expertly forged
    receipts subjected to fine-grained manipulation: digit splicing,
    line-item insertion/deletion, font-weight mismatching, and
    background texture alteration. Ground-truth annotations provide both
    binary fraud labels and spatial bounding boxes ($x,y,w,h$) for
    tampered regions.

## 4. Methodology

We evaluated six state-of-the-art vision-language models: GPT-5.4 Pro,
Grok 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen-2.5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and DeepSeek-v3.
All models were executed at deterministic temperature ($T = 0.0$) with
uniform image input resolution scaling. We compared Direct VLM
Inspection against **Visual-Forensic Chain-of-Thought (VF-CoT)**.

VF-CoT enforces a three-stage visual-semantic reasoning sequence before
declaring a document authentic or manipulated:

1.  **Font & Glyph Geometry Inspection:** Analyze individual characters
    for font family uniformity, stroke-width consistency, character
    spacing (kerning), and baseline alignment across numerical fields.

2.  **Background & Compression Artifact Analysis:** Inspect local
    background texture continuity, shadow gradients, and high-frequency
    edge profiles or compression blocking near key text regions
    (Subtotal, Tax, Total).

3.  **Semantic & Arithmetic Reconciliation:** Perform line-item sum
    verification ($\sum_{}^{}items + tax = total$) to cross-examine
    visual anomalies with logical mathematical constraints.

Performance was measured using **Tampering Classification F1-Score**,
**Bounding Box IoU** (Intersection over Union for localized forged
regions), and **Field Consistency Accuracy**. Statistical differences
were evaluated via Cochran\'s Q test, post-hoc pairwise McNemar\'s tests
with Holm correction ($\alpha = 0.05$), and 1,000-iteration
non-parametric bootstrapping for 95% confidence intervals.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across models, reasoning paradigms, and
dataset contamination levels. On the public FindItAgain-Core benchmark,
GPT-5.4 Pro recorded the highest baseline Tampering Classification F1
(0.86) and localized IoU (0.64). However, on the uncontaminated
ReceiptForge-ZeroDay dataset, all models experienced substantial
performance degradation: GPT-5.4 Pro's F1 dropped to 0.70, while
competing models exhibited 17--23% relative declines.

An omnibus Cochran\'s Q test on the zero-day benchmark indicated
statistically significant differences among the six models
($Cochran'sQ(5) = 42.19,p < .001,Cohen'sw = 0.25$). Post-hoc pairwise
McNemar's tests with Holm correction confirmed that GPT-5.4 Pro
maintained a statistically significant performance lead over other
models under unseen forgery conditions ($adjusted\ p < .05$).

The deployment of VF-CoT yielded universal performance gains on zero-day
evaluation, raising the average Tampering Classification F1 across all
models by $+ 0.13$ and boosting localized region IoU by 28.4%. Error
analysis revealed that direct VLM inspection frequently produced false
negatives on subtle digit replacements that matched overall font style,
whereas VF-CoT successfully detected micro-alignment and arithmetic
discrepancies.

## 6. Discussion

The experimental results demonstrate that while multimodal LLMs possess
strong visual document understanding, reported detection performance on
public benchmarks like FindIt Again is heavily inflated by training set
contamination. The consistent 17--23% performance drop on
ReceiptForge-ZeroDay proves that zero-shot MLLMs struggle with genuine
unseen document forgery detection unless guided by domain-specific
visual forensic protocols.

The efficacy of Visual-Forensic Chain-of-Thought (VF-CoT) highlights a
fundamental characteristic of multimodal reasoning: standard
autoregressive generation favors high-level text transcriptions over
low-level visual pixel inspection. By explicitly requiring models to
inspect glyph kerning, compression boundaries, and arithmetic sums
before declaring a verdict, VF-CoT aligns MLLM attention with the key
diagnostic signals used by human forensic experts.

Limitations of this study include a focus on receipt and invoice
documents, fixed resolution constraints, and deterministic decoding
settings. Future research should expand contamination-free benchmarks to
multi-page contracts, identity documents, and adversarial AI-generated
synthetic document attacks.
