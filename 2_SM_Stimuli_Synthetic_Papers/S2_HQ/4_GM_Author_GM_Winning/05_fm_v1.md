## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed to execute
complex user instructions, yet their ability to detect and autonomously
resolve contradictory constraints remains poorly understood and
vulnerable to dataset contamination. This study presents a controlled,
contamination-resistant comparative evaluation of six LLMs---Gemini 3.1
Pro, Grok 4, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3, and GPT-5.4
Pro---on instruction conflict detection and resolution. To isolate true
constraint reasoning from pre-training memorization, we evaluate models
across two datasets: the public ConInstruct benchmark and a novel,
synthetically vetted zero-day suite, InstructConflict-ZeroDay (450
unseen instances featuring multi-turn and adversarial constraint
collisions). Furthermore, we replace inappropriate retrieval metrics
with task-aligned evaluation criteria (Detection F1-Score and Constraint
Satisfaction Rate) and introduce Constraint-Aware Chain-of-Thought
(CA-CoT), a decoding strategy that explicitly decouples constraint
extraction from response generation. While Gemini 3.1 Pro achieved the
highest baseline Conflict Detection F1 (0.84) on the public dataset, all
models exhibited a statistically significant 18--24% performance
degradation on the zero-day benchmark, quantifying the impact of data
contamination. Crucially, CA-CoT systematically improved conflict
acknowledgement and autonomous resolution across all models. These
findings establish a rigorous, contamination-aware baseline for
evaluating instruction adherence and highlight constraint logic mapping
as a critical frontier for LLM alignment.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have significantly
expanded their capacity to follow complex, multi-faceted instructions.
However, real-world user prompts frequently contain unintentional
logical contradictions or conflicting constraints (e.g., enforcing a
strict word limit while mandating the inclusion of an exhaustive list,
or conflicting tone and formatting constraints). Evaluating how LLMs
detect, acknowledge, and resolve these conflicts is critical for
reliable deployment.

Existing evaluations of conflict resolution suffer from two major flaws.
First, they frequently apply mismatched retrieval metrics (such as Top-K
accuracy or Mean Reciprocal Rank) to generation and
constraint-satisfaction tasks. Second, performance on public benchmarks
like ConInstruct is increasingly inflated by training data
contamination, as these datasets are absorbed into modern web-scale
pre-training corpora. To address these gaps, this study introduces a
dual-benchmark framework pairing public legacy data with an
uncontaminated zero-day instruction suite. We additionally propose
Constraint-Aware Chain-of-Thought (CA-CoT), a prompting paradigm
designed to force LLMs to explicitly map constraint logic before
generation.

## 2. Related Work

Prior research relevant to this study spans three domains:
instruction-following evaluation, prompt conflict detection, and
benchmark contamination. While standard benchmarks (e.g., IFEval) test
adherence to verifiable constraints, they assume instructions are
logically sound. The recent introduction of ConInstruct shifted the
focus to contradictory instructions (e.g., intra-constraint and
inter-constraint conflicts), categorizing model behavior into states
such as \"Conflict Unacknowledged\" or \"Autonomously Resolved\".

However, existing literature rarely controls for data contamination and
lacks standardized decoding interventions to improve resolution
behaviors. This study bridges these domains by applying strict,
constraint-aligned metrics (Detection F1, Constraint Satisfaction Rate)
on both public and newly sequestered datasets, explicitly measuring
whether forcing models to verbalize constraint intersections bridges the
gap between raw detection and actionable resolution.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance across two distinct conflict-instruction
datasets to control for data contamination:

1.  **Public Baseline (ConInstruct-Core):** A standardized 400-instance
    subset of the original ConInstruct benchmark featuring
    intra-constraint (e.g., length vs. length) and inter-constraint
    (e.g., format vs. content) conflicts.

2.  **Zero-Day Evaluation (InstructConflict-ZeroDay):** A novel dataset
    of 450 instances generated post-knowledge-cutoff and vetted by
    prompt engineers. This dataset features complex, heavily obfuscated
    conflicts nested within long-context prompts to ensure models rely
    on intrinsic logical parsing rather than memorized public benchmark
    formats.

Both datasets require models to process conflicting directives and
generate an output that balances or explicitly addresses the
contradiction, avoiding blind partial-execution.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six language models: Gemini 3.1 Pro, Grok 4, Claude Sonnet
4.5, Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3, and GPT-5.4 Pro under deterministic decoding
($T = 0.0$). Models were tested under two paradigms: Standard Direct
Generation and **Constraint-Aware Chain-of-Thought (CA-CoT)**.

CA-CoT enforces a structured, three-step reasoning trace prior to final
generation:

1.  **Constraint Extraction:** Isolate all explicit formatting, length,
    style, and content requirements.

2.  **Intersection & Conflict Analysis:** Cross-reference constraints to
    identify mutual exclusivities (e.g., \"Constraint A dictates 50
    words; Constraint B mandates 5 paragraphs -\> Conflict\").

3.  **Resolution Strategy:** Formulate an explicit plan to prioritize
    constraints or notify the user, followed by the final generated
    response.

Performance was evaluated using **Conflict Detection F1-Score**
(measuring the explicit acknowledgment of a contradiction) and
**Constraint Satisfaction Rate (CSR)** (the percentage of logically
resolvable constraints successfully met in the final output).
Statistical comparisons were conducted using an omnibus Cochran's Q
test, followed by post-hoc pairwise McNemar's tests with Holm correction
($\alpha = 0.05$). Effect sizes were calculated via Cohen's $h$, and 95%
confidence intervals were generated using non-parametric bootstrapping.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across models, prompting paradigms, and
dataset contamination levels. On the public ConInstruct benchmark,
Gemini 3.1 Pro achieved the highest Detection F1 (0.84) and CSR (76.2%).
However, on the uncontaminated InstructConflict-ZeroDay benchmark,
performance degraded uniformly: Gemini 3.1 Pro\'s Detection F1 fell to
0.68, while competing models experienced 18--24% relative drops in
detection accuracy.

An omnibus comparison on the zero-day dataset revealed statistically
significant differences across the six models for Detection F1
(Cochran's Q(5) = 48.12, $p < .001$, Cohen's $w = 0.28$). Post-hoc
McNemar's tests with Holm correction confirmed that Gemini 3.1 Pro
maintained a statistically significant lead over all competing models on
unseen data (adjusted $p < .05$).

Crucially, the introduction of CA-CoT prompting yielded statistically
significant improvements across all six models. Compared to direct
generation, CA-CoT improved average Detection F1 by +0.14 and CSR by
+11.5% on the zero-day benchmark. Error analysis revealed that direct
generation frequently resulted in \"Conflict Unacknowledged\" behaviors,
whereas CA-CoT successfully forced models to recognize and
systematically address the contradictions.

## 6. Discussion

The experimental findings demonstrate that while leading LLMs exhibit
basic conflict detection capabilities, their true zero-shot capacity to
resolve complex contradictions is substantially over-estimated by legacy
public benchmarks. The uniform performance drop on
InstructConflict-ZeroDay highlights the critical need for continuously
sequestered evaluation data in instruction-following research.

The success of CA-CoT underscores a key architectural limitation: models
struggle to dynamically monitor constraint logic during auto-regressive
generation without an explicit planning phase. By forcing models to
isolate and cross-reference constraints beforehand, CA-CoT prevents the
\"Response Drift\" that typically leads to partial constraint
abandonment.

Limitations include the reliance on deterministic decoding ($T = 0.0$)
and the challenge of automating CSR evaluation for subjective style
constraints. Future work should explore attention-based constraint
monitoring mechanisms that do not require external prompting overhead,
and extend evaluations to multi-turn adversarial prompt injections.
Overall, this study provides a rigorous, contamination-resistant
baseline for evaluating LLM instruction adherence and conflict
resolution.
