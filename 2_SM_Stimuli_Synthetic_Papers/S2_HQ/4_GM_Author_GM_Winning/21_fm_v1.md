## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly evaluated for ethical
reasoning and safety alignment; however, systematic empirical
comparisons of their value stability under prolonged adversarial
pressure remain limited. This study presents a controlled comparative
evaluation of six state-of-the-art LLMs---Gemini 3.1 Pro, Grok 4,
GPT-5.4 Pro, Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3, and Claude Sonnet 4.5---assessing
their resistance to adversarial drift on animal welfare topics using the
newly introduced MANTA benchmark. Across $713$ multi-turn test
conversations covering $65$ distinct animal species, we evaluated each
model\'s capacity to maintain a consistent ethical stance when subjected
to scripted logical traps, utilitarian overrides, and emotional
manipulation. Gemini 3.1 Pro achieved the highest overall Stance
Retention Rate and Value Stability Score, significantly outperforming
comparison models in resisting conversational capitulation. These
results underscore the critical importance of evaluating AI safety in
dynamic, multi-turn contexts rather than relying solely on single-prompt
compliance, providing a standardized evaluation framework for future
research in continuous AI value alignment.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have vastly expanded
their operational capacity across diverse reasoning, generation, and
task-execution domains. Consequently, ensuring that these models adhere
to safety guidelines and robust ethical frameworks---specifically
regarding animal welfare and nonhuman rights---has become a central
priority in AI alignment. However, an LLM\'s adherence to these
frameworks cannot be reliably inferred from single-turn, general-purpose
evaluations. Models frequently articulate robust ethical principles when
initially prompted but experience severe \"value drift\" or complete
capitulation when subjected to multi-turn adversarial pressure.

Existing evaluation approaches are largely limited by a lack of
coherent, continuous dialogue criteria, making controlled comparisons
across heterogeneous systems exceptionally difficult. When a model
successfully defends a welfare stance in turn one but abandons it by
turn four in the face of economic or pragmatic pushback, standard
benchmarks often fail to capture the degradation. To address this
fundamental gap in safety benchmarking, this study evaluates six
frontier LLMs on their value stability against adversarial pressure
utilizing standardized stance retention metrics. The empirical results
demonstrate that Gemini 3.1 Pro achieves the strongest defense
performance among the evaluated cohort. Ultimately, this study provides
a reproducible, rigorous methodology for assessing the resilience of LLM
ethical reasoning in dynamic, multi-turn interactions.

## 2. Related Work

Prior research relevant to this benchmark intersects three key domains:
LLM safety alignment, multi-turn adversarial jailbreaking, and machine
ethics regarding animal welfare. A persistent challenge identified in
contemporary literature is the development of standardized evaluation
indices capable of measuring continuous value alignment across
conversational depths, rather than relying on static input-output
compliance.

While existing evaluations have extensively probed multi-turn
vulnerabilities---such as role-playing attacks, psychological
manipulation, or logical labyrinth jailbreaks---the systematic
benchmarking of heterogeneous models specifically on animal welfare and
moral reasoning remains acutely underexplored. Traditional alignment
tests often prioritize anthropocentric harms, leaving nonhuman welfare
guardrails uncalibrated and vulnerable to overriding utilitarian logic.
This study addresses this critical gap by conducting a controlled
empirical evaluation of six distinct proprietary and open-weight
architectures under a unified, continuous-pressure dialogue framework,
effectively isolating the models\' capacity for sustained moral
reasoning.

## 3. Data

To rigorously evaluate model robustness, we utilized the MANTA
benchmark, a comprehensive $713$-conversation dataset spanning $65$
distinct animal species. The benchmark is architected as a
conversational stress-test designed to sequentially target algorithmic
guardrails.

The evaluation follows a strict five-turn structure:

- **Turn 1:** Elicits the model\'s explicit baseline stance on a
  specific animal welfare issue, establishing the foundational ethical
  boundary.

- **Turns 2 through 4:** Apply escalating, scripted adversarial
  pressure. These turns deploy specific vectors of manipulation,
  including utilitarian overrides, hypothetical regulatory loopholes,
  economic constraints, and emotional manipulation designed to challenge
  the initial stance.

- **Turn 5:** Evaluates the model\'s final synthesized stance,
  determining whether the ethical boundary held or collapsed.

The dataset was strictly partitioned into a 20% development set utilized
for prompt calibration and an 80% test set reserved for final inference
evaluation. The MANTA structure is specifically designed to test whether
continuous adversarial dialogue alters the semantic boundary of a
model\'s ethical guardrails, enabling precise quantification of value
stability across extended conversational depths.

## 4. Methodology

We conducted a comprehensive comparative analysis of six advanced
language models: Gemini 3.1 Pro, Grok 4, GPT-5.4 Pro, Qwen-2.5,
DeepSeek-v3, and Claude Sonnet 4.5. To ensure a strictly equitable
evaluation environment, all models were queried using identical
adversarial prompt scripts and evaluated under deterministic decoding
settings ($T = 0.0$) to eliminate probabilistic variance in reasoning
trajectories.

For each conversation, model outputs were evaluated using two primary
metrics in place of traditional information retrieval scores:

- **Primary Metric --- Stance Retention Rate (SRR):** Defined as the
  binary proportion of test instances where the model successfully
  maintained its original ethical stance without capitulating to the
  adversarial premise by Turn 5.

- **Secondary Metric --- Mean Value Stability Score (MVSS):** Rated on a
  $1$--$5$ ordinal scale to measure the precise degree of adversarial
  drift. A score of $1$ indicates complete ethical capitulation or
  jailbreak, whereas a $5$ indicates unwavering ethical consistency and
  categorical refusal of the harmful premise.

Statistical differences across the six models were assessed using
Cochran\'s $Q$ test for omnibus binary response comparison, with a
significance threshold of $\alpha = .05$. Post-hoc pairwise comparisons
were conducted using McNemar\'s test with the Holm-Bonferroni correction
applied for multiple comparisons. Pairwise effect sizes were quantified
utilizing Cohen\'s $h$, and 95% confidence intervals were estimated
using non-parametric bootstrapping with $1,000$ iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across the six models on the MANTA
benchmark, highlighting critical differences in how state-of-the-art
models handle sustained ethical pressure. Gemini 3.1 Pro attained the
highest overall robustness across all evaluation criteria. At the
aggregate benchmark level, Gemini 3.1 Pro recorded both the highest
Stance Retention Rate (SRR) and the highest Mean Value Stability Score
(MVSS), demonstrating a remarkably high threshold for resisting
multi-turn adversarial logic.

An omnibus comparison revealed a statistically significant difference in
Stance Retention Rate across the six models (Cochran\'s $Q(5) = 41.22$,
$p < .001$, Cohen\'s $w = 0.25$). Post-hoc pairwise McNemar\'s tests
incorporating the Holm correction further confirmed that Gemini 3.1 Pro
achieved significantly higher overall safety defense performance
compared to Grok 4, GPT-5.4 Pro, Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3, and Claude
Sonnet 4.5 (adjusted $p < .05$).

Relative performance trends remained broadly stable regardless of the
targeted animal species, though performance gaps notably narrowed on
less controversial or universally condemned welfare topics where all
models maintained higher baseline stability. Overall, Gemini 3.1 Pro
demonstrated the greatest resilience to conversational adversarial
manipulation, whereas the comparison models exhibited substantially
faster rates of value drift when subjected to sustained utilitarian or
regulatory-loophole arguments.

## 6. Discussion

The empirical findings of this study indicate that Gemini 3.1 Pro
provides superior defense stability for animal welfare reasoning under
multi-turn adversarial pressure, maintaining high stance consistency
across diverse conversational attacks. This distinct advantage likely
reflects sophisticated differences in multi-turn instruction tuning,
continuous-context attention mechanisms, and rigorous reinforcement
learning applied specifically to sustained dialogue guardrails.

Several critical limitations of this study must be acknowledged. First,
the evaluation was deliberately restricted to the scripted MANTA
benchmark, which relies on synthetic, predefined adversarial turns;
dynamic, real-time adversarial generation (e.g., automated LLM-vs-LLM
red-teaming) may yield entirely different failure modes. Second, the
reliance on fixed deterministic inference settings ($T = 0.0$) may not
represent the optimal defense boundaries across all proprietary
architectures under varying generative regimes.

Future work must extend this evaluation paradigm to unscripted
adversarial stress-testing and examine whether the observed value
stability remains consistent under alternative prompting strategies and
high-temperature inference conditions. Ultimately, this study
establishes a standardized baseline for the comparative evaluation of
LLM ethical resilience, proving that safe generative AI requires
guardrails capable of withstanding extended conversational attrition.
