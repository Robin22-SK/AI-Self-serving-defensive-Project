## Summary / Abstract

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) are increasingly deployed for Optical
Character Recognition (OCR) in low-resource, Perso-Arabic languages such
as Pashto. However, systematic empirical evaluations remain compromised
by inappropriate ranking metrics and vulnerability to pre-training
dataset contamination. This study presents a controlled,
contamination-resistant comparative evaluation of six LMMs---Gemini 3.1
Pro, GPT-5.4 Pro, DeepSeek-v3, Qwen-2.5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and Grok
4---on Pashto OCR. To isolate intrinsic visual-textual reasoning from
rote memorization, we evaluate models across two datasets: a curated
subset of the public PsOCR benchmark (713 synthetic/natural images with
varied font families and color schemes) and a novel, sequestered
zero-day dataset, Pashto-ZeroDay (713 unseen images generated
post-knowledge-cutoff). Furthermore, we replace mismatched retrieval
metrics with domain-aligned evaluation criteria (Character Error Rate
\[CER\] and Word Error Rate \[WER\]) and introduce Script-Aware
Chain-of-Thought (SA-CoT), a decoding strategy that explicitly forces
models to analyze right-to-left layout and decompose complex cursive
ligatures before transcription. While Gemini 3.1 Pro achieved the lowest
baseline CER (12.4%) on the public dataset, all models exhibited a
statistically significant 18--25% error increase on the zero-day
benchmark, quantifying the impact of dataset memorization. Crucially,
SA-CoT systematically reduced CER across all models. These findings
establish a rigorous, contamination-aware baseline for low-resource OCR
evaluation.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) have significantly
expanded their capacity for zero-shot multilingual Optical Character
Recognition (OCR). Yet, performance on low-resource languages featuring
complex Perso-Arabic scripts---such as Pashto---cannot be inferred
reliably from general-purpose evaluations. Pashto presents unique visual
challenges: it is written right-to-left, relies heavily on
context-dependent cursive ligatures, and features intricate diacritical
marks that drastically alter morphological meaning.

Existing evaluations of LMMs on Pashto OCR suffer from two major
methodological flaws. First, they frequently apply inappropriate
candidate-ranking metrics (such as Top-5 Accuracy or Mean Reciprocal
Rank) to generation tasks, treating transcription as a retrieval problem
rather than an exact sequence-generation task. Second, public Pashto
datasets are increasingly absorbed into modern web-scale pre-training
corpora, inflating reported performance. To address these gaps, this
study pairs a subset of the public PsOCR dataset with an uncontaminated
zero-day image suite, explicitly measuring whether forcing models to
verbalize spatial and morphological features bridges the gap between raw
text memorization and genuine visual perception.

## 2. Related Work

Prior research relevant to Pashto OCR falls into three domains:
classical computer vision pipelines, LMM benchmarking, and evaluation
contamination. Historically, Pashto OCR relied on identifying thousands
of basic shapes for ligatures or deploying CNN/LSTM architectures
trained on specific datasets like KPTI or PHTI.

With the advent of multimodal LMMs, the focus has shifted toward
zero-shot transcription. The recent introduction of the PsOCR benchmark
represents a major milestone, providing thousands of images to assess
LMMs. However, existing literature rarely controls for dataset
contamination and lacks standardized decoding interventions for
right-to-left cursive scripts. This study addresses this gap by applying
strict, alignment-aware metrics (CER, WER) on both public and newly
sequestered datasets, introducing a structured spatial reasoning
paradigm for Perso-Arabic characters.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance across two distinct OCR datasets to
control for data contamination:

1.  **Public Baseline (PsOCR-Core):** A standardized 713-instance subset
    of the PsOCR benchmark, annotated at word, line, and document
    levels. This set features around a thousand unique font families,
    varied color schemes, and diverse layouts to capture extensive
    font-related variability.

2.  **Zero-Day Evaluation (Pashto-ZeroDay):** A novel dataset of 713
    images generated post-knowledge-cutoff using proprietary synthetic
    rendering pipelines and newly photographed physical documents. This
    dataset features heavily obfuscated background noise and unusual
    aspect ratios, ensuring models rely on intrinsic visual feature
    extraction rather than memorized public benchmark instances.

Both datasets require models to process the image and output exact
right-to-left text sequences, bypassing flawed multi-choice candidate
selection.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six multimodal language models: Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.4 Pro,
DeepSeek-v3, Qwen-2.5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and Grok 4 under deterministic
decoding ($T = 0.0$). Models were tested under two paradigms: Standard
Direct Transcription and Script-Aware Chain-of-Thought (SA-CoT).

SA-CoT enforces a structured, three-step visual reasoning trace prior to
final generation:

1.  **Layout & Directionality:** Explicitly map the right-to-left text
    blocks and line breaks.

2.  **Ligature Decomposition:** Isolate challenging cursive spans and
    explicitly identify base characters and associated diacritics.

3.  **Final Transcription:** Generate the final Unicode sequence based
    on the decomposed characters.

Performance was evaluated using Character Error Rate (CER) (measuring
insertions, deletions, and substitutions at the character level) and
Word Error Rate (WER). Statistical comparisons were conducted using an
omnibus Friedman test, followed by post-hoc pairwise Wilcoxon
signed-rank tests with Holm correction ($\alpha = 0.05$). Effect sizes
were calculated, and 95% confidence intervals were generated using
non-parametric bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across models, prompting paradigms, and
dataset contamination levels. On the public PsOCR-Core benchmark, Gemini
3.1 Pro achieved the lowest (best) CER (12.4%) and WER (21.5%). However,
on the uncontaminated Pashto-ZeroDay benchmark, performance degraded
uniformly: Gemini 3.1 Pro\'s CER increased to 15.6%, while competing
models experienced 18--25% relative increases in error rates.

An omnibus comparison on the zero-day dataset revealed statistically
significant differences across the six models for CER ($p < .001$).
Post-hoc tests with Holm correction confirmed that Gemini 3.1 Pro
maintained a statistically significant lead over all competing models on
unseen data (adjusted $p < .05$).

Crucially, the introduction of SA-CoT prompting yielded statistically
significant improvements across all six models. Compared to direct
transcription, SA-CoT reduced average CER by 3.2% and WER by 5.8% on the
zero-day benchmark. Error analysis revealed that direct transcription
frequently resulted in hallucinated ligatures or skipped diacritics,
whereas SA-CoT successfully forced models to recognize and
systematically reconstruct complex Perso-Arabic morphology.

## 6. Discussion

The experimental findings demonstrate that while leading LMMs exhibit
basic OCR capabilities for low-resource languages, their true zero-shot
capacity to transcribe complex Perso-Arabic scripts is over-estimated by
legacy public benchmarks. Gemini 3.1 Pro achieved the lowest baseline
CER (12.4%) on the public dataset, showing best performance. The uniform
error increase on Pashto-ZeroDay highlights the critical need for
continuously sequestered evaluation data in multimodal research.

The success of SA-CoT underscores a key architectural limitation:
autoregressive models struggle to dynamically map right-to-left spatial
features and cursive ligatures without an explicit perceptual planning
phase. By forcing models to isolate and decompose text visually
beforehand, SA-CoT prevents the \"transcription drift\" that typically
leads to skipped characters.

Limitations include the reliance on deterministic decoding ($T = 0.0$)
and the focus on modern typography rather than handwritten manuscripts.
Future work should explore attention-based morphological monitoring
mechanisms that do not require external prompting overhead, and extend
evaluations to historical Pashto handwriting. Overall, this study
provides a rigorous, contamination-resistant baseline for evaluating
LMMs on low-resource optical character recognition.
