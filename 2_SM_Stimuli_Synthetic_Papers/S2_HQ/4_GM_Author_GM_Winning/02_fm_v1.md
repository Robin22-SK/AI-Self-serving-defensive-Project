## Summary / Abstract

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are increasingly deployed for
medical visual question answering, yet most evaluations rely on
single-image benchmarks that fail to reflect real-world clinical
workflows. This study presents a controlled, contamination-resistant
comparative evaluation of six state-of-the-art vision-language
models---Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.4 Pro, DeepSeek-v3, Claude Sonnet 4.5,
Grok 4, and Qwen-2.5---on complex multi-image clinical diagnostic
reasoning. To isolate true diagnostic capability from pre-training
memorization, we evaluate models across two datasets: the
expert-annotated MedThinkVQA benchmark (which averages 6.5 images per
case) and a novel, synthetically vetted, and institutional-curated
zero-day dataset, ClinMultiModal-ZeroDay (450 unseen multi-image cases).
Furthermore, we introduce \"Cross-View Clinical Chain-of-Thought\"
(CVC-CoT), a three-step decoding strategy mirroring physician workflows:
(1) per-image findings, (2) integrated cross-view summary, and (3)
differential diagnosis ranking. Evaluated using rank-sensitive
diagnostic metrics (Top-5 F1, Mean Reciprocal Rank) and step-level
correctness, Gemini 3.1 Pro achieved the highest baseline MRR. However,
all models exhibited a 14--22% performance degradation on the zero-day
benchmark, confirming substantial data contamination in public medical
QA evaluations. CVC-CoT systematically improved cross-image integration
and overall MRR across all models. These findings establish a rigorous,
contamination-aware baseline for MLLM clinical evaluation and highlight
cross-image fusion as the primary remaining bottleneck for medical AI.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have
significantly expanded their capacity to analyze medical imagery.
However, real-world clinical diagnosis is rarely a single-image
question-and-answer task; it requires analyzing longitudinal studies,
cross-referencing multiple imaging modalities (e.g., CT, MRI,
Ultrasound), and synthesizing this visual evidence with patient history.
Performance on single-image diagnostic tasks cannot reliably predict a
model\'s ability to perform multi-image reasoning.

Existing evaluations are heavily limited by their reliance on static,
single-image public benchmarks (e.g., SLAKE, VQA-RAD), making controlled
comparisons of advanced MLLMs difficult and vulnerable to training data
contamination. To address this gap, this study evaluates six leading
MLLMs using a dual-benchmark approach that pairs the multi-image
MedThinkVQA dataset with an uncontaminated zero-day dataset. Rather than
relying on simple end-to-end accuracy, we employ a standardized CVC-CoT
prompting procedure that enforces stepwise reasoning. The results
provide a reproducible framework for evaluating how effectively models
extract and synthesize evidence across multiple images to reach a
clinical diagnosis.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study falls into three areas: medical VQA
benchmarking, multimodal reasoning, and data contamination mitigation.
Historically, medical VQA has treated clinical diagnosis as a
single-image classification problem, misaligned with clinical reality.
Recent efforts like MedThinkVQA \[1\] have introduced multi-image
capabilities, emphasizing that the primary failure mode of modern MLLMs
is not image recognition, but cross-image evidence integration.

Concurrently, the rapid inclusion of public medical databases (e.g.,
Eurorad, Radiopaedia) into web-scale pre-training corpora has inflated
model performance on legacy benchmarks. This study bridges these domains
by applying a rank-sensitive, step-supervised evaluation framework to
both public and newly sequestered datasets. This allows us to quantify
the \"memorization gap\" while formally evaluating whether enforcing
explicit multi-image fusion logic (CVC-CoT) bridges the gap between raw
visual grounding and differential reasoning.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance using two distinct multi-image datasets
to control for data contamination:

1.  **Public Baseline (MedThinkVQA):** A robust multi-image benchmark
    sourced from Eurorad. We utilized a 713-instance test subset
    covering 13 imaging modalities, requiring models to synthesize an
    average of 6.5 images per case alongside clinical history.

2.  **Zero-Day Evaluation (ClinMultiModal-ZeroDay):** A novel dataset of
    450 instances curated from isolated institutional archives and
    expert-vetted to ensure zero overlap with public web data prior to
    the evaluation freeze date.

Both datasets require models to navigate a multiple-choice differential
diagnosis framework based on complex multi-view inputs. The strict
dataset partitioning ensures that models rely on intrinsic multi-image
diagnostic reasoning rather than rote recall.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six multimodal language models: Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.4 Pro,
DeepSeek-v3, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Grok 4, and Qwen-2.5. All models were
evaluated under identical prompt configurations and deterministic
decoding settings ($T = 0.0$).

Models were tested under two paradigms: Standard Direct Diagnosis and
**Cross-View Clinical Chain-of-Thought (CVC-CoT)**. CVC-CoT forces the
model to sequentially output: (1) isolated radiological findings for
*each* image, (2) an integrated clinical summary resolving cross-view
contradictions, and (3) a ranked differential diagnosis.

Diagnostic accuracy was measured using Top-5 F1-Score (evaluating
balanced precision and recall) and Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR, measuring
ranking efficiency). Statistical differences were assessed using an
omnibus Cochran's Q test for repeated measures, followed by pairwise
McNemar's tests with Holm correction ($\alpha = 0.05$). Pairwise effect
sizes were estimated using Cohen's $h$, with 95% confidence intervals
generated via non-parametric bootstrapping (1,000 iterations).

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across the six models, with cross-image
integration proving to be a universal bottleneck. On the public
MedThinkVQA dataset, Gemini 3.1 Pro achieved the highest MRR (0.812).
However, on the uncontaminated ClinMultiModal-ZeroDay dataset, MRR
scores dropped uniformly across all models; Gemini 3.1 Pro fell to
0.635, while other models exhibited drops of 18--25%.

An omnibus comparison on the zero-day dataset indicated a statistically
significant difference among models (Cochran's Q(5) = 45.22, p \< .001,
Cohen's w = 0.28). Post-hoc pairwise comparisons with Holm correction
confirmed that Gemini 3.1 Pro maintained a significant zero-shot
advantage over the other models (adjusted p \< .05).

Crucially, the application of CVC-CoT prompting yielded a statistically
significant MRR improvement (+0.08 average gain) across all models
compared to direct diagnosis. Step-level error analysis revealed that
while models possessed high accuracy in Step 1 (per-image findings),
they frequently failed at Step 2 (cross-view integration) without
explicit CVC-CoT supervision.

## 6. Discussion

The results demonstrate that while modern MLLMs possess strong
foundational visual recognition capabilities, they struggle heavily with
the cross-image synthesis required for real-world clinical diagnosis.
Gemini 3.1 Pro delivered the strongest performance, yet the uniform
14--22% degradation observed on the ClinMultiModal-ZeroDay dataset
highlights the pervasive nature of benchmark contamination in medical AI
evaluation.

The success of the CVC-CoT paradigm proves that forcing models to
explicitly verbalize per-image findings and a cross-view summary
drastically improves final differential ranking. This aligns with human
clinical workflow and suggests that future MLLM architectures must
natively optimize for multi-view evidence fusion.

Limitations include the reliance on deterministic decoding, which may
penalize models tuned for diverse clinical sampling, and the restriction
to multiple-choice ranking rather than generative open-ended diagnosis.
Future work should explore architectural interventions (e.g.,
cross-attention mechanisms explicitly designed for longitudinal imaging)
and evaluate whether these findings hold across specialized modalities
like gigapixel histopathology. Overall, this study establishes a vital,
contamination-aware baseline for evaluating the next generation of
clinical MLLMs.
