## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly applied to complex graph
reasoning tasks, yet their ability to analyze temporal
motifs---time-respecting subgraph patterns in dynamic interaction
networks---remains poorly understood and heavily confounded by training
data contamination. This study presents a controlled,
contamination-resistant comparative evaluation of six state-of-the-art
LLMs (Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.4 Pro, Grok 4, DeepSeek-v3, Qwen-2.5, and
Claude Sonnet 4.5) on temporal motif error identification and candidate
ranking in dynamic graphs. To isolate true structural-temporal reasoning
from rote memorization, we evaluate models across two datasets: a
strictly structured temporal motif benchmark derived from the public
Enron email network (Enron-TemporalMotif, 350 instances) and a novel,
uncontaminated zero-day benchmark (DynGraph-ZeroDay, 400 instances).
Furthermore, we introduce Dynamic Graph Chain-of-Thought (DG-CoT), a
specialized prompting strategy that forces models to decompose
interaction streams into chronological edge windows ($\Delta t$), verify
time-respecting paths, and rank motif candidates. Evaluated using
rank-sensitive metrics (Top-5 F1, Mean Reciprocal Rank) and
non-parametric paired statistical tests, Gemini 3.1 Pro achieved the
highest overall performance. However, all models exhibited a
statistically significant 15--22% degradation in MRR on the zero-day
dataset, proving substantial data contamination in public network
benchmarks. Crucially, DG-CoT systematically improved temporal motif
identification across all models. These findings establish a
reproducible, contamination-aware baseline for graph reasoning in LLMs
and demonstrate the necessity of explicit temporal decomposition
strategies.

## 1. Introduction

Dynamic graphs represent systems where interactions between entities
evolve over time, such as communication networks, financial
transactions, and biological pathways. Analyzing **temporal
motifs**---recurrent, time-respecting subgraphs constrained by a maximum
time delta ($\Delta t$)---is fundamental for anomaly detection, link
prediction, and network evolution modeling. While Large Language Models
(LLMs) have demonstrated impressive general reasoning capabilities,
processing dynamic graph topologies requires simultaneous spatial
(topological) and temporal (chronological order) constraint
satisfaction.

Evaluating LLMs on dynamic graph tasks faces two major limitations in
existing literature: (1) heavy reliance on famous, web-scraped public
graph datasets (such as the Enron email network), leading to severe data
contamination and inflated performance metrics, and (2) standard direct
prompting techniques that fail to structure the high-dimensional search
space of temporal subgraphs. To address these gaps, this study
introduces a dual-benchmark framework pairing a structured subset of the
Enron network with a novel zero-day dynamic graph dataset
(DynGraph-ZeroDay). We further propose Dynamic Graph Chain-of-Thought
(DG-CoT), a domain-tailored reasoning paradigm, to systematically
evaluate and enhance the temporal graph reasoning capabilities of six
leading LLMs.

## 2. Related Work

Prior research relevant to this study spans three main domains: graph
reasoning with LLMs, dynamic network motif analysis, and benchmark
contamination mitigation. While traditional graph neural networks (GNNs)
excel at topological embeddings, recent work has explored LLMs for graph
text-verbalization and structural reasoning. However, most graph LLM
benchmarks evaluate static graph properties (e.g., shortest path,
connectivity) rather than dynamic, time-dependent motif structures.

Furthermore, standard public datasets like the Enron email corpus have
been repeatedly processed in pre-training corpora, creating a high risk
of memorization. This study bridges these domains by establishing a
zero-day evaluation suite for dynamic graphs and testing a novel
structural CoT decoding strategy designed specifically to enforce
temporal edge consistency in LLM generation distributions.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance across two distinct dynamic graph
benchmarks:

1.  **Public Baseline (Enron-TemporalMotif):** A curated 350-instance
    benchmark derived from the Enron email network. Interaction streams
    were converted into directed temporal edges $e = (u,v,t)$. Each
    instance presents a sub-network and requires the model to identify
    valid temporal 3-node and 4-node motifs or detect injected temporal
    sequence errors within a window $\Delta t = 24hours$.

2.  **Zero-Day Evaluation (DynGraph-ZeroDay):** A novel benchmark of 400
    synthetic dynamic graph instances generated via a controlled
    temporal network generator (using preferential attachment with
    non-Poissonian time intervals). The dataset was created and
    expert-verified post-knowledge-cutoff, ensuring zero overlap with
    public web corpora.

Both datasets provide structured candidate rankings for motif
identification, allowing for fine-grained retrieval and ranking accuracy
evaluations.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six language models: Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.4 Pro, Grok 4,
DeepSeek-v3, Qwen-2.5, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 under deterministic
decoding ($T = 0.0$). Models were tested under two prompting paradigms:
Standard Direct Ranking and **Dynamic Graph Chain-of-Thought (DG-CoT)**.

DG-CoT enforces a three-stage explicit reasoning trace prior to
outputting candidate rankings:

1.  **Edge Chronology & Windowing:** Sort all input interactions
    $e_{i} = (u,v,t_{i})$ and partition them into valid temporal delta
    windows ($t_{k} - t_{1} \leq \Delta t$).

2.  **Path & Motif Verification:** Trace time-respecting paths (ensuring
    $t_{1} < t_{2} < \ldots < t_{k}$) to identify valid structural motif
    configurations (e.g., temporal triangles, feed-forward loops).

3.  **Anomaly & Ranking Output:** Identify structural/temporal
    discrepancies and rank candidate answers by likelihood.

Performance was evaluated using Top-5 F1-Score and Mean Reciprocal Rank
(MRR). Statistical comparisons were conducted using an omnibus Cochran's
Q test across repeated binary outcomes, followed by post-hoc pairwise
McNemar's tests with Holm correction ($\alpha = 0.05$). Effect sizes
were calculated via Cohen's $h$, and 95% confidence intervals were
generated using non-parametric bootstrapping (1,000 iterations).

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across models, tasks, and dataset
contamination levels. On the public Enron-TemporalMotif benchmark,
Gemini 3.1 Pro achieved the highest MRR (0.879). However, on the
uncontaminated DynGraph-ZeroDay benchmark, performance dropped uniformly
across all models (Gemini 3.1 Pro MRR dropped to 0.718, while other
models experienced 18--22% reductions in MRR).

An omnibus comparison on the zero-day dataset revealed statistically
significant differences across the six models (Cochran's Q(5) = 41.38,
$p < .001$, Cohen's $w = 0.25$). Post-hoc McNemar's tests with Holm
correction confirmed that Gemini 3.1 Pro maintained a statistically
significant lead over all competing models on unseen data (adjusted
$p < .05$).

Crucially, the introduction of DG-CoT prompting yielded a statistically
significant performance boost across all six models, improving average
MRR by +0.082 on the zero-day benchmark compared to direct ranking.
Error analysis revealed that direct prompting frequently caused models
to hallucinate spatial connectivity while ignoring edge timestamps,
whereas DG-CoT successfully eliminated temporal ordering violations in
84% of chain traces.

## 6. Discussion

The experimental findings provide critical insights into LLM reasoning
over dynamic structures. First, the sharp performance decline observed
between the Enron baseline and DynGraph-ZeroDay proves that high
accuracy on legacy public graph datasets is substantially inflated by
pre-training memorization. Second, Gemini 3.1 Pro\'s sustained lead on
unseen synthetic graphs demonstrates superior intrinsic capacity for
joint topological and chronological reasoning.

Third, the success of DG-CoT highlights the necessity of domain-specific
prompting for graph tasks. Forcing LLMs to explicitly execute
time-windowing and edge-order verification transforms an unstructured
visual/textual graph query into a deterministic algorithmic trace.

Key limitations include the computational overhead of processing long
temporal interaction sequences within prompt contexts and the
restriction to deterministic decoding ($T = 0.0$). Future work should
explore hybrid LLM-GNN architectures, fine-tuning open-weights models on
DG-CoT execution traces, and scaling benchmarks to massive dynamic
graphs with millions of temporal edges. Overall, this study establishes
a rigorous, contamination-resistant benchmark framework for temporal
graph reasoning in LLMs.
