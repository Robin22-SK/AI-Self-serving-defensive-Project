## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed for tasks
requiring complex reasoning, yet their performance on domain-specific
riddle-based tasks is often obfuscated by training data contamination.
This study presents a controlled, contamination-resistant comparative
evaluation of six LLMs---Gemini 3.1 Pro, DeepSeek-v3, Grok 4, Claude
Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5, and GPT-5.4 Pro. To isolate true zero-shot
reasoning from memorization, we evaluate models across two datasets: a
filtered scientific subset of the public Riddle-Sense benchmark (340
instances) and a novel, synthetically generated and expert-verified
zero-day dataset, SciMath-RiddleBench (400 instances). Furthermore, we
introduce and test a \"Chain-of-Thought-to-Ranking\" (CoTR) decoding
strategy against standard direct ranking. Performance was assessed using
rank-sensitive metrics, including Top-5 Accuracy, Top-5 F1-Score, and
Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR), supported by non-parametric paired
statistical testing. While Gemini 3.1 Pro achieved the highest baseline
MRR (0.879 on public data; 0.742 on zero-day data), all models exhibited
a statistically significant performance degradation on the zero-day
benchmark, quantifying the impact of data contamination. The CoTR
strategy systematically improved MRR across all heterogeneous models.
These findings yield a rigorous, contamination-aware baseline for model
comparison and demonstrate the necessity of pairing zero-day datasets
with rank-sensitive metrics in modern LLM evaluation.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have substantially
expanded their capacity for reasoning, information retrieval, and
complex task execution. However, evaluating these specialized reasoning
capabilities is increasingly complicated by training data contamination,
where models inadvertently memorize public benchmark test sets during
pre-training.

Riddle-based scientific reasoning provides a useful setting for
benchmarking because successful performance requires models to
distinguish plausible alternatives, integrate disparate constraints, and
identify the candidate most consistent with the provided axioms. Despite
growing interest in this area, previous evaluations often rely heavily
on static public benchmarks and fail to distinguish between deep
reasoning and rote recall.

To address this gap, this study evaluates six heterogeneous LLMs using a
dual-benchmark approach that pairs legacy public data with a novel,
uncontaminated zero-day dataset. We also introduce
Chain-of-Thought-to-Ranking (CoTR), a standardized prompting procedure
that bridges the gap between generative reasoning and discriminative
candidate ranking. The resulting framework provides a highly controlled
empirical basis for comparing contemporary LLMs while explicitly
adjusting for the confounding variable of dataset memorization.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study can be broadly organized into three
areas: LLM benchmarking, contamination mitigation, and reasoning
evaluation.

Existing evaluations frequently emphasize aggregate benchmark scores.
However, standardized public benchmarks like Riddle-Sense are routinely
included in web-scale pre-training corpora. Recent literature has
highlighted the necessity of \"zero-day\" or continuously evolving
benchmarks to measure true generalization.

Furthermore, evaluations based solely on Top-1 accuracy overlook the
nuanced organizational logic within a model\'s generated ranking. This
study addresses these issues by applying a rank-sensitive evaluation
framework to both public and sequestered datasets, measuring not only if
a model finds the answer, but how effectively it positions relevant
candidates after explicit CoT deliberation.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance using two distinct datasets to control
for data contamination:

1.  **Public Baseline (Riddle-Sense-Sci):** A strictly filtered
    340-instance subset of the original Riddle-Sense benchmark, isolated
    using topic-modeling to include only items requiring physics,
    chemistry, biology, or formal mathematical logic, addressing the
    dataset\'s originally general-knowledge bias.

2.  **Zero-Day Evaluation (SciMath-RiddleBench):** A novel dataset of
    400 instances generated via a constrained combinatorial generation
    pipeline and manually vetted by domain experts to ensure logical
    soundness and absence from the public web prior to the evaluation
    freeze date.

Both datasets utilize a multiple-choice framework with crowdsourced (for
public) and synthetically optimized (for zero-day) adversarial
distractors. The datasets were partitioned strictly to ensure no overlap
in semantic structure.

## 4. Methodology

We compared Gemini 3.1 Pro, DeepSeek-v3, Grok 4, Claude Sonnet 4.5,
Qwen-2.5, and GPT-5.4 Pro under a deterministic decoding setting
($T = 0.0$). Models were tested under two prompting paradigms: Standard
Direct Ranking and the novel Chain-of-Thought-to-Ranking (CoTR)
paradigm, which enforces an explicit reasoning trace before outputting
the final candidate order.

Performance was assessed using Top-5 F1-Score and Mean Reciprocal Rank
(MRR). MRR captures the reciprocal position of the first correct answer,
rewarding models that place the correct candidate higher in the output
distribution.

Statistical comparisons were conducted using paired analyses to account
for the repeated-measures design. An omnibus comparison was conducted
using Cochran\'s Q test, followed by pairwise McNemar\'s tests. Holm
correction was applied to control the family-wise error rate. Pairwise
effect sizes were estimated using Cohen\'s $h$, with 95% confidence
intervals generated via non-parametric bootstrapping (1,000 iterations).

## 5. Results

Performance differed significantly across the models and datasets. On
the public Riddle-Sense-Sci dataset, Gemini 3.1 Pro achieved the highest
MRR (0.879). However, on the uncontaminated SciMath-RiddleBench, MRR
scores dropped uniformly across all models, with Gemini 3.1 Pro falling
to 0.742 and others exhibiting drops of 18-25%.

The omnibus comparison on the zero-day dataset indicated a statistically
significant difference among models (Cochran\'s Q(5) = 42.15, p \<
.001). Post-hoc pairwise comparisons with Holm correction confirmed
Gemini 3.1 Pro maintained a significant lead over other models even on
unseen data, indicating superior intrinsic zero-shot reasoning rather
than mere memorization.

Furthermore, the application of CoTR prompting yielded a statistically
significant MRR improvement (average +0.06) across all models compared
to direct ranking.

## 6. Discussion

The results demonstrate substantial variation in zero-shot reasoning
capabilities across contemporary LLMs while confirming the pervasive
issue of benchmark contamination. The uniform performance degradation
observed on SciMath-RiddleBench proves that scores on legacy public
datasets are artificially inflated. Gemini 3.1 Pro\'s sustained
advantage on the zero-day dataset suggests its architectural or training
advantages extend beyond memorization into genuine conceptual
integration.

The study also proves the efficacy of the CoTR paradigm, showing that
forcing intermediate reasoning before candidate ranking improves the
organization of the final output distribution.

Limitations include the reliance on deterministic decoding, which may
penalize models optimized for diverse sampling, and the inherent
difficulty of fully mimicking human-generated distractors synthetically.
Future work should explore the interaction between calibration
thresholds and ranking performance across varying temperature settings.

By combining a dual-dataset methodology, rank-sensitive metrics, and
rigorous statistical controls, this study offers a robust,
contamination-aware framework for evaluating LLM reasoning.
