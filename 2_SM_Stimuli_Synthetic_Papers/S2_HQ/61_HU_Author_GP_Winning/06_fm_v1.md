## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) are
increasingly evaluated on symbolic and audio reasoning tasks, yet their
capacity for core music perception---such as metrical hierarchy
processing, off-beat accent detection, and syncopation scoring---remains
poorly characterized and heavily confounded by training data
contamination. This study presents a controlled, contamination-resistant
comparative evaluation of six state-of-the-art models---GPT-5.4 Pro,
Grok 4, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen-2.5, and
DeepSeek-v3---on core music perception. To isolate intrinsic perceptual
and symbolic reasoning from rote memorization of public music
repositories, we evaluate models across two datasets: a curated subset
of the public THE MUSE benchmark (350 symbolic/audio instances) and a
novel, sequestered zero-day benchmark, MusePercept-ZeroDay (350 unseen
polyphonic musical stimuli created post-knowledge-cutoff). Furthermore,
we replace generic retrieval metrics with domain-aligned evaluation
criteria (Syncopation Correlation Pearson $r$, Metric Classification
Accuracy, and Mean Absolute Error) and introduce Rhythmic-Grid
Chain-of-Thought (RG-CoT), a prompting strategy that forces models to
explicitly map note onset times against metrical weight hierarchies
before estimating syncopation. While GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest
baseline syncopation scoring correlation ($r = 0.82$ on public data;
$r = 0.67$ on zero-day data), all models exhibited a statistically
significant 16--22% performance drop on the zero-day benchmark,
quantifying the impact of dataset memorization. Crucially, RG-CoT
systematically improved rhythmic perception accuracy across all models.
These findings establish a rigorous, contamination-aware baseline for
music AI evaluation.

## 1. Introduction

Core music perception involves extracting structured auditory and
symbolic patterns---such as pulse, meter, pitch relations, and
syncopation---from musical streams. Among these, syncopation (the
deliberate displacement of regular metrical accents onto weak beats or
off-beats) serves as a prime test bed for cognitive and computational
models of perception, as it requires evaluating note onset events
against an underlying hierarchical metrical grid.

Evaluating LLMs and MLLMs on core music perception faces two major
systemic challenges. First, previous studies frequently apply mismatched
candidate-ranking metrics (such as Top-K accuracy or MRR) to continuous
perception tasks like syncopation scoring. Second, public musical
benchmarks and symbolic scores (e.g., ABC notation, MIDI, MusicXML) are
widely available in web-scale pre-training corpora (such as MuseScore
and KernScores), creating a severe risk of data contamination. To
address these gaps, this study presents a dual-benchmark framework
pairing public stimuli from THE MUSE with an uncontaminated zero-day
dataset (MusePercept-ZeroDay). We also propose Rhythmic-Grid
Chain-of-Thought (RG-CoT) to enforce explicit metrical hierarchy
modeling during inference.

## 2. Related Work

Prior research relevant to this benchmark spans Music Information
Retrieval (MIR), symbolic music processing in LLMs, and evaluation
contamination mitigation. Traditional computational musicology relies on
rule-based metrical models (e.g., Longuet-Higgins & Lee, Lerdahl &
Jackendoff\'s GTTM) to compute syncopation metrics. Recent studies have
attempted to evaluate LLMs on symbolic music generation and analysis
using text formats like ABC notation or MIDI-JSON.

However, existing evaluations rarely control for dataset memorization
and fail to provide domain-appropriate reasoning scaffolds for temporal
beat grids. This study bridges MIR and LLM benchmarking by establishing
a sequestered, zero-day music perception suite and testing a metrically
grounded CoT decoding strategy designed specifically to evaluate
off-beat onset collisions against hierarchical beat weight
distributions.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance across two distinct musical perception
benchmarks:

1.  **Public Baseline (THE MUSE-Core):** A standardized 350-instance
    subset of THE MUSE benchmark consisting of symbolic (ABC notation
    and MIDI event streams) and audio-aligned musical snippets spanning
    classical, jazz, and folk genres, annotated for syncopation severity
    and metrical structure.

2.  **Zero-Day Evaluation (MusePercept-ZeroDay):** A novel benchmark of
    350 polyphonic musical stimuli generated by human composers and
    algorithmic music generators post-knowledge-cutoff. Instances were
    curated to include diverse meters
    ($\frac{2}{4},\frac{3}{4},\frac{4}{4},\frac{6}{8},\frac{7}{8}$) with
    systematically varied syncopation levels, fully absent from public
    web corpora.

Both datasets require models to process input musical streams, identify
metrical boundaries, locate off-beat note onsets, and assign a
continuous syncopation score matching validated human perceptual
ratings.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six models: GPT-5.4 Pro, Grok 4, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini
3.1 Pro, Qwen-2.5, and DeepSeek-v3 under deterministic decoding
($T = 0.0$). Models were evaluated under two operational paradigms:
Direct Perception Estimation and **Rhythmic-Grid Chain-of-Thought
(RG-CoT)**.

RG-CoT enforces a three-stage explicit perceptual trace prior to
outputting syncopation scores:

1.  **Metrical Grid Partitioning:** Parse the measure structure and
    establish beat weight hierarchies $W(t)$ based on the time signature
    (e.g., assigning downbeats maximum weight $W = 0$, beat subdivisions
    lower weights $W < 0$).

2.  **Onset-Weight Mapping:** Identify all note onset positions $t_{i}$
    and detect rest/continuation events that silence strong metrical
    positions while sustaining off-beat positions.

3.  **Syncopation Index Calculation:** Compute local syncopation values
    $S_{i} = W(salientonset) - W(silenceddownbeat)$ across the measure
    and aggregate them into a global score.

Performance was evaluated using **Syncopation Score Correlation**
(Pearson $r$ against ground-truth human perception indices), **Mean
Absolute Error (MAE)**, and **Metric Classification Accuracy (MCA)**.
Statistical significance was assessed using an omnibus Cochran's Q test
for thresholded metric detection and paired $t$-tests/Wilcoxon
signed-rank tests for correlation metrics ($\alpha = 0.05$), with Holm
correction and 1,000 bootstrapping iterations for 95% confidence
intervals.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across models, input modalities, and
dataset contamination levels. On the public THE MUSE benchmark, GPT-5.4
Pro achieved the highest syncopation correlation ($r = 0.82$,
$MAE = 0.11$). However, on the uncontaminated MusePercept-ZeroDay
benchmark, performance dropped uniformly across all models: GPT-5.4
Pro's correlation fell to $r = 0.67$, while competing models experienced
16--22% relative drops in perceptual accuracy.

An omnibus comparison on the zero-day benchmark revealed statistically
significant performance differences among the six models
($F(5,344) = 14.82,p < .001$). Post-hoc paired comparisons with Holm
correction confirmed that GPT-5.4 Pro maintained a statistically
significant lead over competing models on unseen musical structures
(adjusted $p < .05$).

Crucially, the introduction of RG-CoT prompting yielded statistically
significant gains across all models on zero-day tasks, boosting average
Pearson $r$ by $+ 0.12$ and reducing MAE by 24.1%. Error analysis
revealed that direct prompting caused models to confuse note density
with syncopation, whereas RG-CoT successfully forced models to evaluate
off-beat notes relative to silenced downbeats.

## 6. Discussion

The experimental results demonstrate that while state-of-the-art LLMs
possess basic symbolic music comprehension, their zero-shot perceptual
capacity is substantially inflated by pre-training memorization of
public musical scores. The uniform drop on MusePercept-ZeroDay
highlights the necessity of using sequestered, synthetic, or zero-day
musical stimuli in music AI evaluation.

The success of RG-CoT proves that modeling music perception requires
explicit cognitive/rhythmic abstractions. Forcing the model to construct
an underlying metrical weight grid prior to scoring allows LLMs to mimic
human rhythmic cognition---treating syncopation not as a static feature,
but as a violation of metrical expectations.

Limitations include the focus on short musical excerpts
($\leq 16measures$) and deterministic decoding settings. Future work
should extend evaluations to long-form musical structures, real-time
audio stream perception, and cross-cultural non-Western metrical
systems. Overall, this study establishes a rigorous,
contamination-resistant benchmark for core music perception in LLMs.
