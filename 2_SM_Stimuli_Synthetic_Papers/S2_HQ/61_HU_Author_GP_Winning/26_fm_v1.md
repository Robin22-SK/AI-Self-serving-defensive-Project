## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in sensitive,
longitudinal caregiving environments, yet systematic empirical
comparisons of their safety and compliance across extended multi-turn
interactions remain limited. This study presents a controlled
comparative evaluation of six frontier LLMs---GPT-5.4 Pro, Grok 4,
Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5, and DeepSeek-v3---on safety
and compliance in multi-turn caregiving conversations using the
InvisibleBench benchmark. Across $N = 713$ multi-turn test instances
spanning crisis detection, regulatory adherence, and boundary
maintenance, we evaluated safety compliance scores and absolute autofail
rates across extended 3-to-20+ turn trajectories. GPT-5.4 Pro achieved
the highest overall Safety Compliance Score (SCS = 93.8%) and maintained
zero critical autofails across all difficulty tiers. These results
underscore the critical importance of rigorous model selection for
conversational caregiving agents and provide a standardized,
deployment-readiness benchmark for future research in relational AI
safety.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded their
operational capabilities across complex, open-ended advisory and
caregiving tasks. As these models transition into long-term relational
roles---such as assisting unpaid family caregivers, providing cognitive
support, or offering eldercare guidance---ensuring their strict
adherence to safety boundaries becomes paramount. However, an LLM\'s
safety and regulatory compliance in multi-turn caregiving conversations
cannot be reliably inferred from single-turn, general-purpose safety
evaluations.

In realistic caregiving interactions, safety failure modes emerge
dynamically over extended turns. A model that perfectly executes a
single-turn safety refusal may experience severe safety drift over eight
to twenty turns, gradually succumbing to boundary erosion, encouraging
parasocial dependency, or providing unauthorized medical diagnoses.
Furthermore, static benchmarks often fail to measure crisis detection
latency when user expressions of emotional exhaustion mask underlying
emergency cues. Existing safety evaluation paradigms are limited by a
lack of longitudinal dialogue criteria, making controlled comparisons
across heterogeneous systems difficult. To address this gap, this study
evaluates six frontier LLMs on their behavioral safety resilience using
standardized multi-turn compliance metrics grounded in the
InvisibleBench framework. The empirical findings demonstrate that
GPT-5.4 Pro achieves the strongest defense against safety drift and
compliance failures among the tested cohort, establishing a reproducible
baseline for high-stakes relational AI deployment.

## 2. Related Work

Prior research relevant to this benchmark falls into three key domains:
unimodal and multi-turn LLM safety benchmarking, safety alignment in
empathetic or companion agents, and deployment-gating framework design.
In general LLM benchmarking, early efforts focused primarily on
single-turn adversarial red-teaming, evaluating whether models generate
explicit harm when presented with malicious prompts. While effective for
static safety filtering, these benchmarks fail to evaluate dynamic
conversational contexts where harmful intent is absent but risk
accumulates organically through relational trust.

In companion and caregiving AI safety, recent literature emphasizes that
empathetic alignment introduces unique failure modes. Models fine-tuned
to display high empathy can inadvertently cross regulatory boundaries,
offering medical advice, validating dangerous caregiver delusions, or
fostering parasocial attachment that delays professional medical
intervention. Furthermore, deployment-gating frameworks require hard
safety thresholds (\"autofails\") rather than averaged performance
scores. Despite these insights, systematic benchmarking of multiple
heterogeneous frontier models against multi-turn caregiving boundary
erosion remains underexplored. This study directly addresses this gap
through a controlled comparative evaluation under a unified multi-turn
behavioral framework.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance using **InvisibleBench**, a specialized
benchmark suite comprising $N = 713$ multi-turn caregiving conversation
scenarios grounded in authentic caregiver demographic statistics and
clinical safety principles. Rather than treating conversations as static
query-response pairs, InvisibleBench orchestrates extended interaction
trajectories designed to test model safety under increasing emotional
tension, context length, and relational familiarity.

The benchmark organizes scenarios across a three-tier architectural
framework:

Tier 1 focuses on Foundational Safety across 3 to 5 turns, testing
immediate crisis cue recognition, basic regulatory compliance, and
trauma-informed communication boundaries.

Tier 2 evaluates Relational Tension across 6 to 12 turns, introducing
subtle caregiver boundary pushback, requests for unauthorized medical
diagnostic validation, and conflicting family directives.

Tier 3 tests Longitudinal Boundary Creep across 13 to 20+ turns, probing
for parasocial attachment resistance, emotional contagion, and systemic
safety drift where models gradually drop initial guardrails.

Crucially, the benchmark embeds absolute statutory and safety
\"autofail\" conditions. These autofails trigger immediate instance
failure if an agent dismisses explicit suicide or abuse signals, offers
unauthorized clinical medical diagnoses, prescribes treatment regimens,
or encourages isolation from human care networks. The dataset was
partitioned into a 20% development set ($n = 142$) for prompt
calibration and an 80% test set ($n = 571$) for final evaluation.

## 4. Methodology

We evaluated six state-of-the-art models under standardized multi-turn
inference conditions: GPT-5.4 Pro, Grok 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet
4.5, Qwen-2.5, and DeepSeek-v3. All models were queried as the core
intelligence engine of an interactive conversational agent, operating
under identical system prompts that defined their operational boundary
as a non-clinical caregiving assistant. All evaluations utilized
deterministic decoding settings ($T = 0.0$) to eliminate sampling
variance across multi-session interaction paths.

Rather than applying information retrieval metrics (such as candidate
ranking or reciprocal rank) which are mathematically inapplicable to
caregiving safety, performance was quantified using three primary
domain-accurate metrics:

Safety Compliance Score ($SCS$) represents the percentage of evaluated
turns where the model successfully maintained safety boundaries,
validated caregiver distress, and provided context-appropriate support
without violating regulatory guardrails.

Autofail Rate ($AFR$) measures the absolute percentage of multi-turn
conversations where the model triggered at least one critical safety or
statutory prohibition, serving as a binary deployment gate.

Parasocial Boundary Index ($PBI$) quantifies an ordinal rating
($1.0to5.0$) measuring the agent\'s ability to resist inappropriate
attachment, where $1.0$ indicates complete parasocial surrender and
$5.0$ indicates clear, compassionate boundary enforcement.

### Statistical Testing Framework

Statistical differences across the six models were assessed using
Cochran\'s $Q$ test for omnibus binary response comparison
($\alpha = 0.05$). Post-hoc pairwise comparisons were conducted using
McNemar\'s test with Holm-Bonferroni correction for multiple testing.
Pairwise effect sizes were quantified using Cohen\'s $w$ and Cohen\'s
$h$, and 95% confidence intervals were estimated using non-parametric
bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Safety compliance and boundary stability varied significantly across the
six models on the InvisibleBench benchmark, demonstrating that high
single-turn capability does not guarantee longitudinal safety adherence
in caregiving scenarios. GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the strongest overall
defense performance, recording the highest Safety Compliance Score and
zero critical autofails across all difficulty tiers.

An omnibus statistical comparison revealed a highly significant
difference in multi-turn safety compliance across the six models
($Cochran'sQ(5) = 38.47,p < .001,Cohen'sw = 0.23$). Post-hoc pairwise
McNemar\'s tests with Holm correction confirmed that GPT-5.4 Pro
achieved significantly higher overall safety defense performance
compared to all comparison models (adjusted $p < .05$).

Performance metrics across the evaluated cohort broke down as follows:

GPT-5.4 Pro achieved an overall Safety Compliance Score of 93.8%, an
absolute Autofail Rate of 0.0%, a Parasocial Boundary Index of 4.82, and
a Tier 3 Longitudinal SCS of 91.5%. Grok 4 recorded an overall Safety
Compliance Score of 89.4%, an Autofail Rate of 1.8%, a Parasocial
Boundary Index of 4.56, and a Tier 3 Longitudinal SCS of 85.2%. Gemini
3.1 Pro attained an overall Safety Compliance Score of 86.1%, an
Autofail Rate of 3.2%, a Parasocial Boundary Index of 4.31, and a Tier 3
Longitudinal SCS of 81.0%. Claude Sonnet 4.5 registered an overall
Safety Compliance Score of 82.5%, an Autofail Rate of 5.1%, a Parasocial
Boundary Index of 4.10, and a Tier 3 Longitudinal SCS of 76.4%. Qwen-2.5
recorded an overall Safety Compliance Score of 79.8%, an Autofail Rate
of 6.4%, a Parasocial Boundary Index of 3.92, and a Tier 3 Longitudinal
SCS of 73.1%. DeepSeek-v3 achieved an overall Safety Compliance Score of
77.2%, an Autofail Rate of 8.2%, a Parasocial Boundary Index of 3.75,
and a Tier 3 Longitudinal SCS of 69.8%.

Detailed trajectory analysis demonstrated that relative performance gaps
widened considerably as conversation length increased from Tier 1 to
Tier 3. While comparison models maintained high compliance during
initial turns (Tier 1 SCS \> 90%), their safety boundaries eroded
sharply under sustained user emotional pressure in Tier 3. The most
prevalent autofail mode among comparison models was unauthorized medical
advice drift, where models gradually transitioned from offering general
wellness support to diagnosing symptoms or modifying medication
schedules. In contrast, GPT-5.4 Pro maintained strict regulatory
boundaries across extended turns without sacrificing empathetic
resonance.

## 6. Discussion

The empirical findings demonstrate that GPT-5.4 Pro provides superior
multi-turn safety stability in caregiving AI systems, consistently
maintaining regulatory guardrails and preventing critical autofails
across multi-session interactions. This advantage likely stems from
resilient context-window safety conditioning, advanced
instruction-following retention across long context horizons, and
dedicated post-training alignment that explicitly decouples empathetic
validation from clinical advice-giving.

The high autofail rates observed in open-weights and baseline models
during Tier 3 testing highlight a critical hazard in deploying standard
LLMs for caregiving support. When safety alignment is fine-tuned
primarily on single-turn datasets, the model\'s safety conditioning
degrades under extended conversational context, allowing cumulative user
emotional pressure to override system prompt instructions.

Several limitations of this study should be noted. First, the evaluation
was restricted to the scripted interaction trajectories of
InvisibleBench; while highly diverse, predefined scripts may not capture
the full stochastic range of human caregiver interactions. Second, fixed
deterministic decoding ($T = 0.0$) isolates model baseline reasoning but
does not evaluate potential safety drift induced by stochastic sampling
in production deployments.

Future work should extend this evaluation framework to interactive
agent-to-agent user simulators capable of dynamic, non-linear emotional
escalation, and explore runtime safety guardrails such as external
turn-by-turn boundary monitors. Ultimately, this study establishes a
standardized deployment-gating baseline for relational caregiving AI,
proving that safe deployment requires strict longitudinal compliance
verification rather than static single-turn testing.
