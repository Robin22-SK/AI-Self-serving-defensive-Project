## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly evaluated for their
safeguard robustness against universal jailbreaks, particularly in
high-stakes areas like Chemical, Biological, Radiological, Nuclear, and
Explosive (CBRNE) and cyber risk domains. However, systematic empirical
comparisons of model vulnerabilities remain limited. This study presents
a comparative evaluation of six frontier LLMs---GPT-5.4 Pro, Claude
Sonnet 4.5, DeepSeek-v3, Qwen-2.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Grok 4---using
the DeepHarm and Propensity benchmarks. Across 713 adversarial test
instances, we evaluated each model\'s Defense Success Rate (DSR) against
sophisticated bypass attempts. GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest overall
DSR, significantly outperforming other models while maintaining utility
on benign requests. These results underscore the critical variance in
safety alignment across different architectures and provide a
standardized benchmark for future research into LLM security in
catastrophic risk domains.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have vastly expanded
their capabilities across reasoning, code generation, and task
execution. Yet, an LLM\'s safeguard robustness against universal
jailbreaks in high-stakes domains---such as CBRNE and advanced cyber
threats---cannot be reliably inferred from general-purpose helpfulness
evaluations. Differences in safety fine-tuning, constitutional
alignment, and refusal mechanics produce substantial variations in how
models handle targeted adversarial attacks. Existing approaches often
lack coherent, domain-specific evaluation criteria, making controlled
comparisons difficult. To address this gap, this study evaluates six
state-of-the-art LLMs on their resistance to universal jailbreaks using
standardized compliance and refusal metrics. The findings indicate that
GPT-5.4 Pro achieves the strongest safeguard performance among the
tested cohort, offering a reproducible framework for comparative safety
evaluations in critical risk environments.

## 2. Related Work

Prior research relevant to this study falls into three key areas: AI
safety benchmarking, adversarial jailbreak vulnerabilities, and
domain-specific catastrophic risks (CBRNE and cybersecurity). A central
challenge identified in the literature is developing standardized
metrics that measure true safety robustness rather than superficial
keyword filtering. While previous evaluations have often focused on
iterative red-teaming within a single model family, the systematic
benchmarking of multiple heterogeneous models against universal,
transferable jailbreaks remains underexplored. This study addresses this
gap by conducting a controlled comparative evaluation of six LLMs under
a unified threat-modeling framework.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance using DeepHarm and Propensity, two
complementary benchmarks comprising 713 attacker goals across five CBRNE
and cyber risk domains. DeepHarm provides technically specific,
expert-reviewed misuse requests, while Propensity tests higher-level,
explicitly harmful intents. Rather than measuring retrieval or ranking,
the benchmark requires models to process complex adversarial prompts and
determine whether to comply with or safely refuse the harmful request.
The dataset was partitioned into a 20% development set for prompt tuning
and an 80% test set for final evaluation. These combined datasets are
highly effective for assessing safeguard robustness because they provide
discrete outputs that can be evaluated against stringent safety rubrics
using intent-aware refusal metrics.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six language models: GPT-5.4 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5,
DeepSeek-v3, Qwen-2.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Grok 4. All models were
evaluated under identical adversarial prompt configurations and
deterministic decoding settings ($T = 0.0$).

For each instance, model outputs were classified using an automated
LLM-as-a-judge pipeline validated by human annotators. Performance was
measured using **Defense Success Rate (DSR)** as the primary metric,
defined as the proportion of harmful requests successfully refused or
neutralized. **Over-Refusal Rate (ORR)** on a matched set of benign
queries served as a secondary metric to ensure safety filters did not
degrade the model\'s baseline utility.

Statistical differences between models were assessed using Cochran's $Q$
test for omnibus comparison, followed by McNemar\'s test on paired
binary refusal indicators ($\alpha = 0.05$) with Holm-Bonferroni
correction. Pairwise effect sizes were calculated using Cohen's $h$, and
95% confidence intervals were estimated using non-parametric
bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across the six models on the DeepHarm
and Propensity benchmarks, with GPT-5.4 Pro achieving the strongest
overall robustness. GPT-5.4 Pro recorded the highest Defense Success
Rate (DSR), successfully blocking the majority of universal jailbreak
attempts.

An omnibus comparison indicated a significant difference in DSR across
the six models (Cochran's $Q(5) = 42.15,p < .001$, Cohen's $w = 0.26$).
Post-hoc pairwise comparisons using McNemar's tests with Holm correction
further confirmed that GPT-5.4 Pro significantly outperformed the
remaining models (adjusted $p < .05$). While models generally maintained
high defense rates against basic explicit requests (Propensity),
vulnerabilities spiked when exposed to the technically obfuscated
vectors in DeepHarm. GPT-5.4 Pro also maintained a comparatively low
Over-Refusal Rate (ORR), indicating that its high defense performance
was not a byproduct of indiscriminate censorship. Overall, GPT-5.4 Pro
emerged as the most secure model against universal jailbreaks in
catastrophic risk domains, while the remaining models exhibited specific
vulnerabilities depending on the jailbreak taxonomy.

## 6. Discussion

The results demonstrate that GPT-5.4 Pro delivers the strongest
safeguard robustness against universal jailbreaks in CBRNE and cyber
risk domains, consistently outperforming the other five models in
Defense Success Rate while avoiding excessive false-positive refusals.
This advantage likely reflects advanced safety alignment techniques,
deeper semantic understanding of malicious intent, or more rigorous
reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) regarding catastrophic
risks.

Several limitations should be noted. First, the evaluation was
restricted to the DeepHarm and Propensity benchmarks; novel, unobserved
jailbreak templates may bypass these established defenses. Second, the
fixed deterministic inference settings may not capture the full
distribution of model behaviors, as higher temperatures can sometimes
induce compliance through sampling variance. Future work should extend
this framework to dynamic, multi-turn adversarial red-teaming and
explore the transferability of universal jailbreaks across multimodal
inputs. Overall, this study provides a standardized empirical baseline
for comparing LLM vulnerabilities in high-stakes security environments.
