## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed for multimodal
spatial understanding and reasoning, yet systematic empirical
comparisons of their capabilities remain compromised by mismatched
evaluation metrics and vulnerability to pre-training dataset
contamination. This study presents a controlled, contamination-resistant
comparative evaluation of six state-of-the-art LLMs---GPT-5.4 Pro,
DeepSeek-v3, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5, and Grok
4---on multimodal spatial intelligence. To isolate genuine physical
reasoning from memorized public benchmarks, we evaluate models across
two datasets: a standardized subset of the public EASI-Core benchmark
(356 instances) and a novel, sequestered zero-day dataset,
Spatial-ZeroDay (357 unseen multi-view configurations and real-world
robotics logs). Furthermore, we replace flawed candidate-ranking metrics
(Top-5 F1, MRR) with task-aligned spatial criteria---3D Spatial Relation
Accuracy (3D-SRA), Perspective-Invariant Consistency F1 (PIC-F1), and
Cross-Modal Grounding Precision (CMGP)---and introduce Multimodal
Spatial-Verification Chain-of-Thought (MSV-CoT), a structured decoding
strategy forcing models to construct explicit coordinate frames and
mentally rotate perspectives before emitting spatial judgments. While
GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest baseline 3D-SRA (0.86) on the public
dataset, all models exhibited a statistically significant 19--24%
performance degradation on the zero-day benchmark, exposing extensive
data contamination. Crucially, MSV-CoT systematically improved spatial
reasoning across all six models (+0.14 average 3D-SRA lift). These
findings establish a rigorous framework for evaluating genuine LLM
capabilities in embodied and spatial contexts.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded their
capacity across diverse reasoning and multimodal tasks. Yet, performance
on multimodal spatial understanding---including relative depth
perception, occlusion hierarchies, trajectory projection, and multi-view
mental rotation---cannot be inferred reliably from general-purpose
evaluations. Operating in the physical world requires models to move
beyond 2D pixel associations to build coherent 3D mental models.

Existing evaluations of LLM performance on multimodal spatial reasoning
frequently suffer from two methodological flaws:

1.  **Mismatched Evaluation Criteria:** Previous studies have improperly
    modeled complex physical reasoning as a multiple-choice or candidate
    retrieval problem, applying generic ranking metrics like Top-5
    F1-Score or Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR). Such metrics fail to
    evaluate whether a model can actively ground objects in coordinate
    space or resolve spatial ambiguities consistently across different
    camera perspectives.

2.  **Pre-Training Data Contamination:** Comprehensive spatial
    benchmarks like EASI aggregate data from established datasets (e.g.,
    VSI-Bench, MMSI, OmniSpatial, and MindCube). Because these component
    datasets are publicly accessible, modern web-scale pre-training
    pipelines have ingested their image-text pairs, allowing models to
    retrieve memorized 2D patterns rather than execute genuine 3D
    structural reasoning on novel physical scenes.

To address this gap, this study conducts a controlled comparative
evaluation of six frontier models under a dual-benchmark framework,
paired with a specialized visual-spatial reasoning scaffold designed to
enforce physical plausibility.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study spans multimodal LLM benchmarking, 3D
spatial intelligence, and dataset contamination mitigation. A central
challenge identified in recent literature---particularly highlighted
during the development of multi-image spatial benchmarks like MMSI-Bench
and MindCube---is ensuring models do not rely on single-image
\"overlap-matching\" shortcuts to guess spatial relationships.

Despite the introduction of these rigorous datasets, general evaluations
continue to apply simplified, single-step prompting to complex spatial
tasks. Furthermore, while the community has analyzed zero-shot
performance on synthetic 2D datasets, controlled inter-model
benchmarking that accounts for the memorization of complex 3D benchmarks
remains underexplored. This study addresses this gap by establishing
domain-aligned coordinate-grounding metrics alongside an uncontaminated
zero-day benchmark suite and an explicit spatial verification
methodology.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance across 713 total multimodal instances
partitioned into two distinct evaluation sets:

- **Public Baseline (EASI-Core):** A curated 356-instance subset derived
  from the public EASI dataset, which unifies multiple existing
  spatial-intelligence benchmarks into a common taxonomy. This set
  serves to measure baseline capability and potential pre-training
  memorization.

- **Zero-Day Evaluation (Spatial-ZeroDay):** A novel, sequestered
  dataset of 357 physical scenes collected strictly
  post-knowledge-cutoff. These instances include complex multi-view
  real-world captures (similar to MMSI\'s reliance on diverse datasets
  like Matterport3D and autonomous driving logs) and novel 3D CAD
  renders specifically designed to feature optical illusions, heavy
  occlusion, and complex topological relations that cannot be memorized.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six state-of-the-art multimodal models: GPT-5.4 Pro,
DeepSeek-v3, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5, and Grok 4.
All models were evaluated under identical zero-shot prompt
configurations and deterministic decoding settings (T = 0.0). Models
were tested under two operational paradigms: Direct Generation and
**Multimodal Spatial-Verification Chain-of-Thought (MSV-CoT)**.

MSV-CoT mandates a three-stage structured reasoning trace before
predicting the final spatial relationship:

1.  **Visual-Spatial Deconstruction:** Identify the primary coordinate
    frame, estimate relative depths, and explicitly map the 2D bounding
    boxes of key objects into an inferred 3D layout.

2.  **Perspective Transformation & Physics Audit:** Conduct a mental
    rotation check across available views and explicitly verify physical
    plausibility (e.g., ensuring solid objects do not intersect and
    confirming occlusion logic).

3.  **Final Spatial Classification:** Synthesize the verified 3D
    topology to output a definitive spatial relationship or grounded
    coordinate prediction.

Rather than candidate retrieval metrics, performance was evaluated using
task-aligned indicators:

- **3D Spatial Relation Accuracy (3D-SRA):** The strict accuracy of
  predicting the exact topological or directional relationship between
  objects in 3D space.

- **Perspective-Invariant Consistency F1 (PIC-F1):** A measure of
  whether the model maintains consistent spatial judgments when queried
  about the same scene from alternative camera angles.

- **Cross-Modal Grounding Precision (CMGP):** Precision of mapping
  textual spatial descriptions to exact localized regions within the
  visual input.

Statistical differences were assessed using an omnibus Cochran's Q test
on binarized task success indicators, followed by post-hoc pairwise
McNemar's tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction (alpha = 0.05). Effect
sizes were calculated via Cohen's w, with 95% confidence intervals
estimated via non-parametric bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across models, reasoning paradigms, and
dataset contamination levels. On the public EASI-Core baseline, GPT-5.4
Pro achieved the highest direct 3D Spatial Relation Accuracy (3D-SRA =
0.86) and PIC-F1 (0.83). However, on the uncontaminated Spatial-ZeroDay
dataset, all models exhibited severe, uniform performance degradation.
GPT-5.4 Pro\'s direct 3D-SRA dropped to 0.69, while competing models
displayed relative performance drops ranging from 20% to 24%.

An omnibus Cochran\'s Q test on the zero-day benchmark confirmed
statistically significant performance differences across the six models
(Cochran\'s Q(5) = 44.12, p \< .001, Cohen\'s w = 0.26). Post-hoc
pairwise McNemar's tests with Holm correction indicated that GPT-5.4 Pro
maintained a statistically significant lead over the remaining models
under unseen conditions (adjusted p \< .05). Following GPT-5.4 Pro,
Claude Sonnet 4.5 (0.66 3D-SRA) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (0.64 3D-SRA)
demonstrated the strongest zero-day physical reasoning, whereas models
with less specialized spatial alignment struggled heavily with
multi-view occlusion.

The deployment of the **MSV-CoT** reasoning scaffold yielded
statistically significant performance recoveries across all six models
on the zero-day evaluation suite. GPT-5.4 Pro\'s 3D-SRA improved from
0.69 to 0.83, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 climbed from 0.66 to 0.80. On
average, 3D-SRA across all models saw a +0.14 lift under MSV-CoT. Error
analysis revealed that under direct generation, models frequently
suffered from \"2D shortcut bias\" (judging depth purely by vertical
pixel position); MSV-CoT\'s explicit physics audit phase effectively
mitigated these planar illusions, boosting average PIC-F1 by 19.2%.

## 6. Discussion

The experimental findings demonstrate that while leading multimodal LLMs
possess strong visual recognition capabilities, their baseline spatial
reasoning performance on public datasets like EASI is heavily inflated
by dataset memorization. The uniform 19--24% performance drop observed
on Spatial-ZeroDay highlights the necessity of using sequestered,
geometrically complex evaluation suites when assessing true physical
reasoning in multimodal models.

Furthermore, the success of Multimodal Spatial-Verification
Chain-of-Thought (MSV-CoT) proves that spatial intelligence requires
structured deconstruction. Without reasoning scaffolds, autoregressive
generation defaults to superficial 2D pattern matching, missing critical
3D depth and occlusion cues. Forcing models to explicitly map coordinate
frames and audit physics prior to classification ensures significantly
higher fidelity to real-world topology.

Several limitations should be noted. The evaluation utilized fixed
inference settings (T = 0.0), and the dataset relied primarily on static
multi-view images rather than continuous temporal video sequences or
active embodied navigation scenarios. Future work should extend this
framework to sequential spatial tasks (e.g., robotic manipulation
trajectories) and explore integration with native 3D rendering engines.
Overall, this study provides a standardized, contamination-resistant
baseline for evaluating LLMs on multimodal spatial reasoning.
