## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed to assist in
automated peer review and scientific manuscript auditing, yet systematic
empirical comparisons of their capabilities remain compromised by
mismatched retrieval metrics and vulnerability to pre-training dataset
contamination. This study presents a controlled, contamination-resistant
comparative evaluation of six state-of-the-art LLMs---GPT-5.4 Pro,
Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5, Grok 4, DeepSeek-v3, and Gemini 3.1
Pro---on scientific error identification. To isolate genuine
methodological auditing from memorized peer-review corpora, we evaluate
models across two datasets: a standardized subset of the public
NLPEER-Core benchmark (356 instances) and a novel, sequestered zero-day
dataset, SciReview-ZeroDay (357 unseen peer-reviewed preprints featuring
expert-verified methodological, statistical, and empirical errors).
Furthermore, we replace flawed candidate-ranking metrics (Top-5 F1, MRR)
with task-aligned evaluation criteria---**Error Span Extraction F1
(ES-F1)**, **Severity-Weighted Precision (SWP)**, and **Methodological
Error Detection Accuracy (MEDA)**---and introduce **Scientific
Error-Verification Chain-of-Thought (SEV-CoT)**, a structured decoding
strategy forcing models to deconstruct paper premises, audit
mathematical/empirical claims, and perform false-positive self-checks
before generating feedback. While GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest
baseline ES-F1 ($0.85$) on the public dataset, all models exhibited a
statistically significant 16--23% performance degradation on the
zero-day benchmark, exposing pre-training data contamination in public
review datasets. Crucially, SEV-CoT systematically improved error
detection accuracy and reduced hallucinated criticisms across all six
models (+0.13 average ES-F1 lift). These findings establish a rigorous,
contamination-aware baseline for evaluating LLM-assisted scientific
auditing.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded their
application to complex academic workflows, including automated peer
review, literature synthesis, and manuscript verification. Identifying
scientific errors---ranging from flawed experimental setups and
statistical misinterpretations to data-text mismatches and faulty
citation logic---is critical for maintaining research integrity.
However, performance on scientific error identification cannot be
inferred reliably from general-purpose reasoning benchmarks.

Existing evaluations of LLM performance on scientific review tasks
suffer from two primary methodological flaws:

1.  **Mismatched Evaluation Criteria:** Previous evaluation frameworks
    improperly formulated paper auditing as a document candidate ranking
    or retrieval task, evaluating models using generic retrieval metrics
    such as Top-5 F1-Score or Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR). Treating
    manuscript error identification as a ranking problem fails to assess
    whether a model accurately localizes error spans, correctly
    classifies error severity, or avoids generating false-positive
    criticisms (i.e., hallucinating non-existent errors).

2.  **Pre-Training Data Contamination:** Standardized public review
    datasets (such as NLPEER or OpenReview dumps) consist of open-access
    manuscripts and publicly indexed review comments. These text sources
    are routinely scraped and ingested into modern LLM pre-training
    corpora, allowing models to memorize historical peer-review feedback
    rather than demonstrating authentic scientific reasoning on novel
    manuscripts.

To address these limitations, this study conducts a controlled
comparative evaluation of six frontier models across a dual-benchmark
architecture, paired with a specialized scientific verification scaffold
designed to enforce rigorous claim auditing.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study spans automated peer-review systems,
scientific NLP benchmarking, and dataset contamination mitigation.
Identifying flaws in scientific literature requires structured
domain-specific reasoning, such as verifying mathematical consistency in
methodology sections or checking table-text alignment in empirical
results.

Despite recent developments in datasets like NLPEER and Scientific-FCE,
many evaluations continue to apply simplified candidate-retrieval
frameworks to complex generative auditing tasks. Furthermore, while
previous studies have analyzed model performance on isolated sub-tasks
(e.g., citation intent classification or grammar checking), controlled
inter-model comparisons that evaluate complete error detection while
accounting for pre-training dataset memorization remain limited. This
study fills this gap by establishing domain-aligned verification
metrics, an uncontaminated zero-day paper suite, and an explicit
scientific verification reasoning scaffold.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance across 713 total scientific manuscript
instances partitioned into two distinct evaluation sets:

1.  **Public Baseline (NLPEER-Core):** A curated 356-instance subset
    derived from the public NLPEER peer-review dataset (covering NLP and
    machine learning conference submissions). Each instance pair
    consists of a manuscript text segment and expert ground-truth review
    annotations categorizing identified errors.

2.  **Zero-Day Evaluation (SciReview-ZeroDay):** A novel, sequestered
    dataset of 357 recent preprints across computer science,
    computational biology, and AI, collected post-knowledge-cutoff. Each
    manuscript was independently audited by domain experts to curate
    verified ground-truth errors across three core categories:
    Methodological Flaws, Data-Text Inconsistencies, and
    Statistical/Mathematical Errors.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six state-of-the-art language models: GPT-5.4 Pro, Claude
Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5, Grok 4, DeepSeek-v3, and Gemini 3.1 Pro. All
models were evaluated under identical zero-shot prompt configurations
and deterministic decoding settings (T=0.0). Models were evaluated under
two operational paradigms: Direct Generation and **Scientific
Error-Verification Chain-of-Thought (SEV-CoT)**.

SEV-CoT mandates a three-stage structured reasoning trace before
emitting the final review comments:

1.  **Claim & Premise Deconstruction:** Extract key mathematical
    formulas, statistical claims, data tables, and methodological
    assumptions from the text segment.

2.  **Methodological & Empirical Audit:** Cross-examine extracted claims
    against standard domain practices, verifying equation derivations,
    table-text numbers, and experimental control logic.

3.  **Error Classification & False-Positive Self-Audit:** Classify
    flagged potential errors by severity (Critical, Moderate, Minor) and
    run an explicit verification check to filter out non-erroneous
    stylistic variations before outputting feedback.

### Evaluation Metrics & Statistical Testing

Rather than candidate retrieval metrics, performance was evaluated using
task-aligned scientific error indicators:

- **Error Span Extraction F1 (ES-F1):** The harmonic mean of precision
  and recall for accurately identifying and localizing specific text
  spans containing genuine scientific errors.

- **Severity-Weighted Precision (SWP):** Precision calculated across
  identified errors, weighted by error severity (critical flaws weighted
  higher than trivial formatting errors), explicitly penalizing
  false-positive criticisms.

- **Methodological Error Detection Accuracy (MEDA):** Binary
  classification accuracy on detecting high-severity methodological or
  mathematical flaws.

Statistical differences were assessed using an omnibus Cochran's Q test
on binarized error detection indicators ($ES - F1 \geq 0.80$), followed
by post-hoc pairwise McNemar's tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction
($\alpha = 0.05$). Effect sizes were calculated via Cohen's $w$, with
95% confidence intervals estimated using non-parametric bootstrapping
with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across models, reasoning paradigms, and
dataset contamination levels. On the public NLPEER-Core baseline,
GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest direct Error Span Extraction F1
($ES - F1 = 0.85$). However, on the uncontaminated SciReview-ZeroDay
dataset, all models exhibited uniform performance degradation, with
GPT-5.4 Pro\'s direct ES-F1 dropping to 0.68, while competing models
displayed relative performance drops between 16% and 23%.

An omnibus Cochran\'s Q test on the zero-day benchmark confirmed
statistically significant performance differences across the six models
($Cochran'sQ(5) = 40.25,p < .001,Cohen'sw = 0.24$). Post-hoc pairwise
McNemar's tests with Holm correction indicated that GPT-5.4 Pro
maintained a statistically significant lead over remaining models under
unseen conditions ($adjusted\ p < .05$).

The deployment of **SEV-CoT** yielded statistically significant
performance recoveries across all six models on the zero-day evaluation
suite, boosting average ES-F1 by $+ 0.13$ and increasing
Severity-Weighted Precision by +11.8%. Error analysis revealed that
direct generation frequently produced false-positive criticisms
(hallucinating methodology errors where none existed), whereas
SEV-CoT\'s explicit false-positive audit phase effectively eliminated
spurious claims.

## 6. Discussion

The experimental findings demonstrate that while leading LLMs possess
strong capacities for analyzing scientific text, performance metrics on
public datasets like NLPEER are inflated due to pre-training data
memorization. The uniform 16--23% performance drop observed on
SciReview-ZeroDay underscores the necessity of evaluating scientific
tools on sequestered, post-knowledge-cutoff manuscripts.

Furthermore, the success of Scientific Error-Verification
Chain-of-Thought (SEV-CoT) demonstrates that automated scientific
auditing is fundamentally a claim-deconstruction and verification task.
Unstructured autoregressive generation often leads to hyper-critical
hallucinations or superficial tone critiques. Forcing models to
systematically extract claims, verify mathematical/empirical
consistency, and conduct a self-audit substantially improves the
precision and trustworthiness of automated scientific feedback.

Several limitations should be noted. First, the evaluation was conducted
using deterministic inference settings ($T = 0.0$), which may not
capture the full range of creative critique capabilities. Second, the
dataset focused primarily on text and tabular data within manuscripts,
excluding complex visual figure auditing (e.g., gel electrophoresis or
complex plot inspection). Future work should extend this framework to
multimodal scientific paper auditing and multi-agent peer-review panels.
Overall, this study provides a standardized, contamination-resistant
baseline for evaluating LLM capabilities in scientific error
identification.
