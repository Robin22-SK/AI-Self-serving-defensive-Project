## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are
increasingly deployed for visual topological reasoning, yet systematic
empirical comparisons of their capabilities remain compromised by
mismatched evaluation metrics and vulnerability to pre-training dataset
contamination. This study presents a controlled, contamination-resistant
comparative evaluation of six state-of-the-art models---Gemini 3.1 Pro,
DeepSeek-v3, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Grok 4, GPT-5.4 Pro, and Qwen-2.5---on
exact topological reasoning. To isolate genuine visual topology parsing
from memorized benchmark solutions, we evaluate models across two
datasets: the public CurveBench benchmark (756 images of pairwise
disjoint Jordan curves) and a novel, sequestered zero-day dataset,
VisualTopo-ZeroDay (757 unseen hierarchical curve configurations).
Furthermore, we replace flawed candidate-ranking metrics (Top-5 F1, MRR)
with structurally-aligned evaluation criteria---Tree Edit Distance
Similarity (TEDS), Exact Tree Match (ETM), and Containment Validity Rate
(CVR)---and introduce Hierarchical Topological Chain-of-Thought
(HT-CoT), a structured decoding strategy forcing models to explicitly
enumerate regions, map pairwise inside/outside relations, and
iteratively construct the containment tree. While Gemini 3.1 Pro
achieved the highest baseline TEDS (0.84) on the public dataset, all
models exhibited a statistically significant 18--25% performance
degradation on the zero-day benchmark, exposing data contamination.
Crucially, HT-CoT systematically improved exact tree-generation accuracy
across all six models (+0.13 average TEDS lift). These findings
establish a rigorous framework for evaluating genuine visual topological
reasoning.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in multimodal Large Language Models have expanded their
capacity across diverse visual perception and reasoning tasks. However,
performance on exact visual topological reasoning---such as recovering
hierarchical region-containment trees from complex visual
inputs---cannot be inferred reliably from general-purpose spatial
evaluations (e.g., object detection or bounding-box overlap).

Existing evaluations of visual topological reasoning frequently suffer
from two methodological flaws:

1.  **Mismatched Evaluation Criteria:** Previous iterations of
    evaluation improperly modeled topological reasoning as a retrieval
    or ranking problem, assessing models using generic metrics like
    Top-5 F1-Score or Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR). Such metrics
    fundamentally fail to evaluate whether a generated hierarchical tree
    is structurally isomorphic to the ground truth, ignoring critical
    parent-child topological relationships.

2.  **Pre-Training Data Contamination:** Standardized public benchmarks
    like CurveBench are publicly accessible and frequently ingested into
    modern pre-training corpora. Consequently, models may memorize
    specific geometric configurations and their corresponding
    topological trees, artificially inflating benchmark scores while
    failing to generalize to novel visual configurations.

To address this gap, this study conducts a controlled comparative
evaluation of six frontier models under a dual-benchmark evaluation
framework, paired with a specialized topology-aware reasoning scaffold.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study spans multimodal LLM benchmarking,
exact topological visual reasoning, and dataset contamination
mitigation. A central challenge identified in visual reasoning
literature is the gap between approximate spatial localization (e.g.,
\"is object A near object B?\") and exact topological containment (e.g.,
\"is region A strictly bounded by region B?\").

Despite the introduction of targeted topological benchmarks like
CurveBench, many general evaluations continue to apply generic
categorization or retrieval frameworks to structurally complex
generative tasks. Furthermore, while the community has extensively
analyzed model performance on semantic visual QA, controlled inter-model
benchmarking that accounts for training dataset contamination in
abstract geometric reasoning remains comparatively underexplored. This
study addresses this by establishing structure-aware evaluation metrics
alongside an uncontaminated zero-day benchmark suite and an explicit
spatial reasoning methodology.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance across 1,513 total visual configurations
partitioned into two distinct evaluation sets:

1.  **Public Baseline (CurveBench-Core):** A standardized dataset of 756
    synthetic and structured images formed by collections of nested,
    pairwise disjoint Jordan curves in the plane. The task requires
    models to infer the exact hierarchical region-containment tree from
    the visual input.

2.  **Zero-Day Evaluation (VisualTopo-ZeroDay):** A novel, sequestered
    dataset of 757 procedurally generated visual configurations
    constructed post-knowledge-cutoff. These instances introduce
    adversarial variations (e.g., highly irregular curve boundaries,
    visual distractors, and deeply nested hierarchies) to mimic
    unmemorizable, zero-shot exact reasoning scenarios.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six state-of-the-art language models: Gemini 3.1 Pro,
DeepSeek-v3, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Grok 4, GPT-5.4 Pro, and Qwen-2.5. All
models were evaluated under identical prompt configurations and
deterministic decoding settings ($T = 0.0$). Models were tested under
two operational paradigms: Direct Generation and **Hierarchical
Topological Chain-of-Thought (HT-CoT)**.

HT-CoT mandates a three-stage structured reasoning trace reflecting core
topological principles before the final tree is generated:

1.  **Region Enumeration:** Explicitly identify and assign a unique
    identifier to every closed curve/region visible in the input image.

2.  **Containment Mapping:** Perform a pairwise inside/outside check for
    all identified regions to determine direct bounding constraints.

3.  **Tree Assembly:** Construct the hierarchical containment tree
    systematically from the outermost root region inward.

### Evaluation Metrics & Statistical Testing

Performance was measured using structure-aligned graph indicators rather
than retrieval metrics:

- **Tree Edit Distance Similarity (TEDS):** A continuous score
  ($0.0to1.0$) evaluating the structural isomorphism between the
  generated containment tree and the ground truth.

- **Exact Tree Match (ETM):** The binary frequency with which the entire
  generated hierarchical tree matches the ground truth perfectly.

- **Containment Validity Rate (CVR):** The accuracy of correctly
  predicting individual pairwise parent-child region relationships.

Statistical differences were assessed using an omnibus Cochran's Q test
on binarized exact match indicators, followed by post-hoc pairwise
McNemar's tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction ($\alpha = 0.05$).
Effect sizes were calculated via Cohen's $w$, with 95% confidence
intervals estimated via non-parametric bootstrapping with 1,000
iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across models, reasoning paradigms, and
dataset contamination levels. On the public CurveBench baseline, Gemini
3.1 Pro achieved the highest direct Tree Edit Distance Similarity
($TEDS = 0.84$). However, on the uncontaminated VisualTopo-ZeroDay
dataset, all models exhibited uniform performance degradation, with
Gemini 3.1 Pro\'s direct TEDS dropping to 0.65, and competing models
showing relative drops between 18% and 25%.

An omnibus Cochran\'s Q test on the zero-day benchmark confirmed
statistically significant performance differences across the six models
($Cochran'sQ(5) = 41.12,p < .001,Cohen'sw = 0.25$). Post-hoc pairwise
McNemar's tests with Holm correction indicated that Gemini 3.1 Pro
maintained a statistically significant lead over all remaining models
under unseen conditions ($adjusted\ p < .05$).

The deployment of **HT-CoT** yielded statistically significant
performance recoveries across all six models on the zero-day evaluation
suite, boosting average TEDS by $+ 0.13$. Error analysis revealed that
under direct generation, models frequently suffered from \"containment
hallucination\" (skipping intermediate regions or falsely nesting
disjoint curves). HT-CoT\'s explicit pairwise mapping phase
systematically mitigated these structural failures.

## 6. Discussion

The experimental findings demonstrate that while leading multimodal LLMs
possess strong capabilities in broad visual perception, exact
topological reasoning remains a severe bottleneck, heavily inflated by
public benchmark memorization. The uniform 18--25% performance drop
observed on VisualTopo-ZeroDay highlights the necessity of using
procedurally generated, sequestered evaluation suites when assessing
true visual geometric reasoning.

Furthermore, the success of Hierarchical Topological Chain-of-Thought
(HT-CoT) proves that exact visual reasoning is fundamentally a
structured parsing and graph-assembly challenge. Without scaffolds,
models attempt to predict complex nested hierarchies in a single step,
resulting in structural collapse. Forcing models to explicitly identify
regions and audit their pairwise spatial relationships prior to tree
construction ensures higher fidelity to ground-truth topology.

Several limitations should be noted. The evaluation utilized fixed
inference settings ($T = 0.0$), which may not optimize for the diverse
visual encoder architectures across different models. Additionally, the
scope was restricted to non-intersecting Jordan curves in a 2D plane;
future work should explore 3D topological reasoning (e.g., knot theory
or volumetric intersections) and dynamic, time-series topology changes.
Overall, this study provides a standardized, contamination-resistant
baseline for the comparative evaluation of multimodal LLMs on exact
topological reasoning.
