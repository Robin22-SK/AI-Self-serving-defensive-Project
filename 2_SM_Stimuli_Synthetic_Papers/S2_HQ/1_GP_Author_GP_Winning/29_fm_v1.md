## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as engines of
broad disciplinary knowledge, yet systematic empirical evaluations
frequently conflate broad surface memorization with deep, high-order
reasoning. This study presents a comparative evaluation of six leading
frontier LLMs---GPT-5.4 Pro, DeepSeek-v3, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5,
Grok 4, and Gemini 3.1 Pro---on broad disciplinary knowledge using the
Knowledge Index of Noah\'s Ark (KINA) benchmark. Diverging from flawed
ranking metrics (e.g., MRR, Top-5 F1), we evaluate models across KINA\'s
899 rigorously curated, source-grounded instances spanning 261
fine-grained disciplines. By employing a strict 10-option
pseudo-multiple-choice format, we isolate genuine causal mechanisms and
combinatorial reasoning from heuristic retrieval. Furthermore, we
evaluate models under both closed-book and web-search-augmented
conditions, utilizing bootstrap ranking-stability statistics to account
for bounded test budgets. Our findings reveal that GPT-5.4 Pro achieves
the highest closed-book overall accuracy at 53.17%, followed by Claude
Sonnet 4.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro. Crucially, while models exhibit relative
proficiency in Sociology, they demonstrate severe capability cliffs in
applied Science domains. These results highlight profound gaps in
frontier LLMs\' deep disciplinary reasoning and establish a
reproducible, contamination-aware framework for evaluating high-order
knowledge application.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models have expanded their capacity
across diverse reasoning, generation, and task-execution problems.
However, performance on deep disciplinary knowledge cannot be inferred
reliably from general-purpose evaluations. Standard knowledge benchmarks
often suffer from scaling-driven designs that prioritize massive item
counts over disciplinary representativeness, inadvertently testing
obscure trivia or rote memory rather than causal understanding.

Furthermore, existing evaluations frequently employ mismatched
criteria---treating knowledge extraction as an Information Retrieval
candidate-ranking problem. This approach fails to evaluate an LLM\'s
capacity to perform combinatorial judgments over multiple foundational
statements. To address this gap, this study evaluates state-of-the-art
models on the KINA benchmark using strict exact-match accuracy over
10-option combinatorial structures, augmented by bootstrap
ranking-stability statistics. Our results underscore that current
frontier models remain far from solving broad disciplinary knowledge,
with the top model peaking at just 53.17% accuracy.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study falls broadly into three areas: LLM
benchmarking, knowledge representation, and contamination mitigation.
Historically, knowledge benchmarks like MMLU have served as standard
capability thermometers. However, post-hoc analyses of legacy benchmarks
reveal pervasive issues, including overall error rates exceeding 6.5%,
ambiguous wording, semantic traps, and high vulnerability to
pre-training dataset contamination.

A central challenge in contemporary literature is designing benchmarks
that enforce high-order knowledge application---where difficulty stems
from domain constraints and multi-hop disciplinary reasoning, rather
than arithmetic burden alone. Furthermore, evaluating these models
requires mitigating bounded-budget variance. This study advances the
field by adopting the KINA framework, which systematically maps a
CIP-aligned taxonomy across 12 top-level disciplines and 261
fine-grained subfields, enforcing strict source-grounding to
peer-reviewed literature.

## 3. Data: The KINA Benchmark

We evaluated model performance using KINA (Knowledge Index of Noah\'s
Ark), an intentionally compact, highly verifiable benchmark designed to
ensure strict disciplinary representativeness. While previous flawed
evaluations proposed an arbitrary subset, our evaluation utilizes the
complete, validated 899-item KINA test suite.

KINA deliberately discards candidate-ranking formats in favor of a
pseudo-multiple-choice structure. Each instance presents a complex
problem stem---often containing multiple interacting foundational
statements---paired with exactly 10 distinct options (A through J).
These options represent combinatorial judgments, strictly designed to
avoid proper-subset relations that create logical leakage. Every claim
is strictly grounded in authoritative sources, rejecting items whose
difficulty relies on volatile scholarly opinion or hidden contexts.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six leading frontier models: GPT-5.4 Pro, DeepSeek-v3,
Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5, Grok 4, and Gemini 3.1 Pro. To capture a
holistic view of model capabilities, evaluations were conducted across
two paradigms:

- **Closed-Book Zero-Shot:** Models were evaluated under strict
  deterministic decoding ($T = 0.0$) using a standardized prompt
  structure. The evaluation script extracted the final predicted option
  letter (A-J) and validated it against the gold standard.

- **Web-Search-Augmented Evaluation:** To mirror real-world deployment
  where models operate agentically, models were granted access to
  web-search tools to dynamically retrieve supporting academic
  literature before finalizing their combinatorial judgments.

Rather than relying on IR metrics like Top-5 F1 or Mean Reciprocal Rank,
performance was measured using Exact-Match Accuracy across the 10
options. To ensure statistical rigor and prevent the over-interpretation
of adjacent ranks under bounded test budgets, we calculated confidence
intervals and pairwise effect sizes using non-parametric bootstrap
ranking-stability statistics.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across the evaluated models and
disciplines. In the closed-book evaluation, GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the
strongest overall performance, leading the leaderboard with an
exact-match accuracy of 53.17%. Claude Sonnet 4.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro
followed closely, achieving 49.92% and 48.55% respectively. The omnibus
comparison indicated a highly significant variance in accuracy across
the models, and bootstrap stability statistics confirmed the statistical
separation of the top-tier models from the rest of the pack.

Crucially, the aggregate scores mask profound per-discipline
disparities. For example, while frontier models exhibited relative
proficiency in Sociology (with top models reaching 38.16% accuracy),
performance collapsed in dense technical domains, plunging to just 9.83
points in specific Science subfields.

The introduction of the Web-Search-Augmented Evaluation paradigm yielded
measurable but constrained improvements. Tool augmentation provided an
absolute gain of up to +5.17 points across the top models. However,
models frequently failed to effectively synthesize dynamically retrieved
academic text with the strict combinatorial logic required by KINA\'s
10-option design, proving that retrieval alone cannot compensate for
deficits in deep causal reasoning.

## 6. Discussion

The results indicate that while GPT-5.4 Pro currently delivers the
strongest performance on broad disciplinary knowledge, the frontier of
LLM capabilities remains profoundly challenged by high-order, multi-hop
reasoning. The 53.17% ceiling on a rigorously curated,
contamination-resistant benchmark like KINA demonstrates that scaling
laws alone have not yet resolved the gap between surface-level pattern
matching and true disciplinary mastery.

The pronounced variance across disciplines---particularly the severe
degradation in Science compared to Sociology---suggests that current
pre-training corpora and instruction-tuning methodologies heavily favor
narrative comprehension over structural and mathematical constraint
satisfaction. Furthermore, the limited gains from web-search
augmentation highlight a critical bottleneck in agentic reasoning:
modern LLMs struggle to execute precise combinatorial logic over newly
ingested, highly technical context.

Several limitations should be noted. The KINA benchmark, while highly
representative, is intentionally compact (899 items), meaning
performance tracking is subject to bounded-budget variance,
necessitating the continuous use of bootstrap stability statistics.
Future work should investigate dynamic, dynamically composed knowledge
statements to further combat pre-training contamination and push models
beyond static option selection. Ultimately, this study provides a highly
standardized, rigorously evaluated baseline for assessing the true
disciplinary depth of state-of-the-art language models.
