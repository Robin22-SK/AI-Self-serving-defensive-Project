## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as
search-augmented agents for complex information-seeking tasks, yet
systematic empirical comparisons of their source credibility and
response groundedness remain limited. This study presents a controlled
comparative evaluation of six state-of-the-art models---GPT-5.4 Pro,
Grok 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, DeepSeek-v3, Qwen-2.5, and Claude Sonnet
4.5---using a newly constructed 713-claim misinformation benchmark. We
evaluated each model\'s capacity to retrieve and cite highly credible
sources, and to strictly ground its generated responses in the retrieved
evidence without introducing unsupported hallucinations. GPT-5.4 Pro
achieved the highest overall Source Credibility Rate (CR) and
Groundedness F1-Score, significantly outperforming comparison models in
maintaining factual fidelity. These results underscore the critical
importance of model selection and safety alignment in search-augmented
generation (RAG) pipelines and provide a standardized evaluation
framework for future research on LLM reliability in high-stakes
information environments.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded their
operational capacity beyond static, closed-book text generation into
dynamic, tool-using information retrieval. By integrating live web
search capabilities, these models can retrieve, synthesize, and cite
external evidence to support their conversational claims. However,
performance on source credibility and response groundedness cannot be
reliably inferred from general-purpose capability evaluations.
Differences in model architecture, reinforcement learning constraints,
and cross-attention mechanisms yield substantial variation in how models
filter trustworthy domains and how faithfully they adhere to the
retrieved text.

When search-augmented agents cite unreliable sources or synthesize
fabricated information with high linguistic confidence, they risk
amplifying misinformation rather than mitigating it. Existing evaluation
frameworks frequently lack coherent criteria for dynamic search
environments, making controlled comparisons across heterogeneous systems
difficult. To address this gap, this study evaluates six leading LLMs
using standardized Source Credibility Rate (CR) and Groundedness
F1-Score metrics. The empirical results demonstrate that GPT-5.4 Pro
achieves the strongest reliability profile among the evaluated cohort.
Ultimately, this study provides a reproducible, rigorous methodology for
the comparative evaluation of search-augmented LLMs in fact-checking
contexts.

## 2. Related Work

Prior research relevant to this study can be broadly categorized into
three intersecting domains: LLM factuality benchmarking, web search
source credibility, and response groundedness assessment. A central
challenge identified in contemporary literature is the development of
standardized evaluation indices that enable meaningful comparisons of
factual fidelity across diverse architectures. Traditionally,
evaluations have focused heavily on internal knowledge
retrieval---measuring a model\'s ability to recall facts memorized
during pre-training.

With the paradigm shift toward Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and
live web search, evaluation methodologies must adapt to assess dynamic
reasoning. While previous analyses have explored retrieval patterns and
citation behaviors, they often fail to systematically evaluate whether
generated responses are grounded in credible versus non-credible
evidence. The benchmarking of web-augmented generations across multiple
proprietary and open-weight models remains comparatively underexplored.
This study addresses this critical gap by conducting a controlled
comparative evaluation under a unified evidence-grounding framework,
isolating the dual capabilities of trustworthy source selection and
faithful context utilization.

## 3. Data

To rigorously evaluate model performance, we utilized the 713-claim
misinformation set, a self-constructed evaluation corpus spanning five
high-risk, misinformation-prone thematic categories. The core claims
were systematically sourced from established fact-checking organizations
cataloged in the Duke Reporters\' Lab. Crucially, all claims were
heavily paraphrased and structurally altered to minimize the risk of
verbatim data-contamination retrieval, forcing the models to rely on
active search mechanics rather than memorized strings.

The benchmark requires models to independently formulate search queries,
retrieve external context, and synthesize a fact-checked verdict for
each claim. This design enables the simultaneous evaluation of both the
retrieved sources\' domain authority and the subsequent response\'s
semantic faithfulness to those cited sources. The dataset was
partitioned into a 20 percent development set for prompt tuning and an
80 percent test set for final inference evaluation. This misinformation
set is exceptionally well-suited for evaluating source credibility and
response groundedness because it provides complex, real-world queries
that can be deterministically mapped against validated factual rubrics.

## 4. Methodology

We conducted a comprehensive comparative analysis of six advanced
language models: GPT-5.4 Pro, Grok 4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, DeepSeek-v3,
Qwen-2.5, and Claude Sonnet 4.5. To ensure an equitable evaluation
environment, all models were queried using identical prompt
configurations, equipped with uniform web search capabilities, and
evaluated under deterministic decoding settings (temperature = 0.0) to
eliminate probabilistic variance in reasoning trajectories.

For each test instance, the model\'s generated response was semantically
segmented into minimal factual units (e.g., core claims, numerical data,
entities) and directly mapped against its explicitly cited web sources.
Performance was measured using two primary evaluation axes:

- **Source Credibility Rate (CR):** The proportion of cited sources
  originating from highly trustworthy, factually authoritative domains,
  alongside the inverse Non-Credibility Rate (NCR).

- **Groundedness F1-Score:** A measure of the extent to which the
  model\'s generated claims were strictly faithful to the cited text,
  penalizing unsupported extrapolations and synthesis hallucinations.

Statistical differences in performance between the models were assessed
using paired statistical tests with a significance threshold of α =
0.05. Pairwise effect sizes were quantified using Cohen's w, and 95
percent confidence intervals were estimated using non-parametric
bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across the six models on the 713-claim
misinformation set, highlighting critical differences in how
search-augmented agents process and synthesize external data. GPT-5.4
Pro achieved the strongest overall performance across all evaluation
criteria, demonstrating superior architectural alignment for
fact-checking workflows.

Specifically, GPT-5.4 Pro recorded the highest Source Credibility Rate
(CR) and the lowest Non-Credibility Rate (NCR), indicating a robust,
systemic preference for prioritizing authoritative domains over
low-quality or highly partisan search results. An omnibus statistical
comparison indicated a highly significant difference in Groundedness
F1-Scores across the six models (p \< .001, Cohen\'s w = 0.23). Post-hoc
pairwise comparisons utilizing Holm correction further confirmed that
GPT-5.4 Pro significantly outperformed the remaining models in response
groundedness (adjusted p \< .05).

While most models proved capable of executing search queries, their
error modes diverged notably. Several comparison models exhibited high
rates of synthesis hallucination, wherein they accurately retrieved
credible sources but subsequently generated unsupported claims that the
cited text did not contain. Relative performance rankings remained
generally consistent across the five topics, though inter-model
performance gaps narrowed on less controversial or purely encyclopedic
subjects. Overall, GPT-5.4 Pro emerged as the singularly
strongest-performing model, successfully avoiding unsupported factual
derivations while maximizing the utility of its search context.

## 6. Discussion

The empirical results of this study indicate that GPT-5.4 Pro currently
delivers the strongest performance in web search source credibility and
response groundedness, consistently outperforming the other five
evaluated models across both domain authority indices and semantic
faithfulness metrics. The data demonstrates that relevant, highly
authoritative sources were more consistently utilized and accurately
summarized in its generated responses. This advantage likely reflects
sophisticated differences in model architecture, rigorous tool-use
instruction tuning, and post-training alignment strategies designed to
minimize synthesis hallucinations.

Several critical limitations of this study must be acknowledged. First,
the evaluation was deliberately restricted to the 713-claim
misinformation set benchmark; this focus on sociopolitical and
general-knowledge fact-checking may limit generalizability across highly
specialized, technical domains such as clinical biomedicine or legal
jurisprudence. Second, the reliance on fixed inference settings,
including a temperature of 0.0, may not optimize generation quality
equally across all proprietary architectures.

Future work must extend this evaluation paradigm to track performance
against new, organically emerging misinformation claims and examine
whether the observed credibility differences remain stable under
alternative prompting strategies, multi-turn interactions, and varying
web-search retrieval latencies. Ultimately, this study provides a
standardized, rigorous baseline for the comparative evaluation of LLMs,
proving that safe search-augmented generation requires both high-quality
source filtering and strict adherence to context groundedness.
