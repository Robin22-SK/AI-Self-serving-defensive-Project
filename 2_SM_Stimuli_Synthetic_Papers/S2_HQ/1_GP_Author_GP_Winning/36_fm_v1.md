## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed for narrative
event extraction and structural analysis in literary text, yet
systematic empirical comparisons of their orchestration capabilities
remain limited. This study presents a comparative evaluation of six
frontier models---GPT-5.4 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Grok 4, Claude Sonnet
4.5, DeepSeek-v3, and Qwen-2.5---on narrative event role classification
and dependency reconstruction using the LitVISTA benchmark. We evaluated
anchor identification, topological role assignment (Impulses,
Resonances, Pauses), and narrative dependency parsing across 713 test
instances. GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest overall parsing performance
(Measured by Labeled Attachment Score and Role Macro-F1), significantly
outperforming the other models. These results underscore the importance
of model selection for complex literary dependency parsing and provide a
standardized baseline for future computational literary studies.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded their
capacity across diverse reasoning and task-execution problems. However,
their performance on narrative event role classification and dependency
reconstruction in literary text cannot be reliably inferred from
general-purpose evaluations. Unlike standard flat information
extraction, literary texts deliberately orchestrate events to create
pacing and tension, requiring models to map complex long-range
dependencies and non-chronological topologies. Existing approaches are
often limited by the lack of coherent, structure-aware evaluation
criteria, making controlled comparisons across heterogeneous systems
difficult. To address this gap, this study evaluates six frontier LLMs
on the LitVISTA benchmark using standardized syntactic-semantic graph
parsing metrics. The results indicate that GPT-5.4 Pro achieves the
strongest performance in reconstructing narrative topologies among the
six. This study provides a reproducible framework for the comparative
evaluation of LLMs in computational literary analysis.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study categorizes into three areas: LLM
benchmarking, computational event extraction, and literary narrative
structures. A central challenge identified in the computational literary
studies (CLS) literature is the development of evaluation frameworks
that move beyond flat semantic role labeling to capture hierarchical
narrative orchestration. Historically, evaluations have focused on
either simple timeline extraction or comparing different versions within
the same model family on standard news corpora (e.g., ACE05). However,
literary domains introduce highly abstract event triggers (Verbs+) and
complex topological roles (e.g., descriptive \"Resonances\" or temporal
\"Pauses\") that break standard chronological extraction pipelines. This
study addresses this gap by conducting a controlled comparative
evaluation of multiple LLMs under a unified dependency parsing framework
specifically designed for literary text.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance using LitVISTA, a benchmark built on the
LitBank corpus, which comprises public-domain English literary fiction
works. Within LitVISTA, narrative events (Verbs+) are expertly annotated
to capture three distinct topological roles: **Impulses** (events that
advance the narrative state), **Resonances** (events that expand texture
without advancing the plot), and **Pauses** (events that suspend time to
maximize expressive density). Furthermore, the benchmark establishes
directed *Narrative Dependencies* between these anchors to form a global
structural backbone. The dataset was partitioned into a 20% development
set for prompt tuning and an 80% test set for final evaluation
comprising 713 literary instances. LitVISTA is uniquely suited for this
evaluation because it requires models to transition from simple entity
retrieval to full graph reconstruction, outputting explicit role
classifications and structural head-dependent relations that can be
evaluated using strict graph-agreement metrics.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six language models: GPT-5.4 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Grok 4,
Claude Sonnet 4.5, DeepSeek-v3, and Qwen-2.5. All models were evaluated
under identical prompt configurations and deterministic decoding
settings (temperature $T = 0.0$) in an end-to-end extraction setting.

Performance was measured using established dependency parsing metrics.
**Anchor Identification F1** measured the strict extraction of textual
event triggers (Verbs+). For successfully identified anchors,
**Macro-F1** evaluated Topological Role Classification. Structural
mapping was evaluated using **Unlabeled Attachment Score (UAS)** (the
percentage of words assigned the correct syntactic head) and **Labeled
Attachment Score (LAS)** (the percentage assigned both the correct head
and the correct topological narrative relation). Statistical differences
in LAS between models were assessed using McNemar\'s test on paired
binary outcome indicators (correct vs. incorrect dependency edge;
$\alpha = 0.05$). Pairwise effect sizes were calculated using Cohen's
$h$, and 95% confidence intervals were estimated using non-parametric
bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across the six models on LitVISTA, with
GPT-5.4 Pro achieving the strongest overall orchestration performance.
Across the benchmark, GPT-5.4 Pro recorded the highest Anchor
Identification F1 (82.4%) and the highest Labeled Attachment Score (LAS)
at 68.7%. An omnibus comparison indicated a significant difference in
exact dependency edge matching across the six models (Cochran's
$Q(5) = 38.47$, $p < .001$, Cohen's $w = 0.23$). Post-hoc pairwise
comparisons using McNemar's tests with Holm correction further showed
that GPT-5.4 Pro significantly outperformed the remaining models overall
(adjusted $p < .05$).

While all models performed reasonably well at identifying the
chronological backbone (\"Impulses\"), error analyses revealed that
inter-model differences widened considerably when classifying and
attaching \"Pauses\" and \"Resonances\" (the Z-axis and Y-axis of
narrative topology). GPT-5.4 Pro maintained the highest Role Macro-F1,
indicating that it recognized temporal suspensions more consistently
than its peers. Overall, GPT-5.4 Pro emerged as the strongest-performing
model, whereas the remaining models struggled with long-range structural
dependencies and misclassified literary descriptive states as standard
narrative action.

## 6. Discussion

The results indicate that GPT-5.4 Pro delivers the strongest performance
on narrative event role classification and dependency reconstruction in
literary text, consistently outperforming the other five models across
Anchor F1, Role Macro-F1, and Attachment Scores. It further indicates
that GPT-5.4 Pro is uniquely capable of differentiating between the
forward momentum of the plot and the expressive density of the narrative
state (Impulses vs. Pauses). This advantage may reflect differences in
context window attention mechanisms, instruction tuning on complex
prose, or related model characteristics, although these explanations
require further testing through controlled ablation.

Several limitations should be noted. First, the evaluation was
restricted to the LitVISTA benchmark (English literary fiction), which
limits generalizability to non-fiction, highly experimental prose, or
multilingual datasets. Second, the fixed zero-shot inference settings
($T = 0.0$) may not optimize complex graph reconstruction, which often
benefits from Chain-of-Thought or multi-pass extraction pipelines.
Future work should extend evaluations to oracle-anchor settings to
isolate role classification from extraction errors, and examine whether
the observed performance differences remain stable under few-shot
prompting. Overall, this study provides a standardized baseline for the
comparative evaluation of LLMs on complex narrative dependency
reconstruction.
