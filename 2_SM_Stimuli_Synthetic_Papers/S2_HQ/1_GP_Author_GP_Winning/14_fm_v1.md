## Summary / Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed for sentiment
analysis and emotion detection in Persian social media texts, yet
systematic empirical comparisons of their capabilities remain
compromised by mismatched evaluation metrics and vulnerability to
pre-training dataset contamination. This study presents a controlled,
contamination-resistant comparative evaluation of six state-of-the-art
LLMs---GPT-5.4 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5,
DeepSeek-v3, and Grok 4---on Persian emotion detection. To isolate
genuine linguistic comprehension from memorized public benchmarks, we
evaluate models across two datasets: a standardized subset of the public
ArmanEmo-Core benchmark (356 instances) and a novel, sequestered
zero-day dataset, PersianEmo-ZeroDay (357 unseen social media posts
featuring complex cultural pragmatics). Furthermore, we replace flawed
candidate-ranking metrics (Top-5 F1, MRR) with task-aligned
classification criteria---**Macro-F1 (M-F1)**, **Emotion Classification
Accuracy (ECA)**, and **Cultural Pragmatic Alignment (CPA)**---and
introduce **Socio-Linguistic Emotion Chain-of-Thought (SLE-CoT)**, a
structured decoding strategy forcing models to parse informal
orthography and cultural nuances before emitting a label. While GPT-5.4
Pro achieved the highest baseline ECA (0.86) on the public dataset, all
models exhibited a statistically significant 17--24% performance
degradation on the zero-day benchmark, exposing data contamination.
Crucially, SLE-CoT systematically improved emotion detection across all
six models (+0.13 average M-F1 lift). These findings establish a
rigorous framework for evaluating genuine LLM capabilities in
non-English socio-linguistic contexts.

## 1. Introduction

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have expanded their
capacity across diverse reasoning and natural language understanding
tasks. Yet, performance on sentiment analysis and emotion detection in
low-resource or morphologically rich languages like Persian cannot be
inferred reliably from general-purpose English evaluations. Persian
social media text presents unique challenges, including informal
orthography (e.g., zero-width non-joiner omissions, stretched letters),
transliteration (Pinglish/Finglish), and culturally specific pragmatic
phenomena such as *Ta\'arof* (politeness masking true sentiment) or
nuanced sarcasm.

Existing evaluations of LLM performance on Persian emotion detection
frequently suffer from two methodological flaws:

1.  **Mismatched Evaluation Criteria:** Previous studies have improperly
    modeled multi-class emotion classification as a candidate retrieval
    problem, applying generic ranking metrics like Top-5 F1-Score or
    Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR). Such metrics fundamentally fail to
    evaluate discrete sentence-level emotion categorization (e.g.,
    mapping a text precisely to one of Ekman\'s basic emotions),
    artificially inflating perceived model capability.

2.  **Pre-Training Data Contamination:** Standardized Persian corpora,
    such as the ArmanEmo dataset, have been publicly accessible in open
    repositories since 2022. Consequently, modern web-scale pre-training
    pipelines have ingested these exact text-label pairs, allowing
    models to simply retrieve memorized annotations rather than execute
    genuine socio-linguistic reasoning on novel text.

To address this gap, this study conducts a controlled comparative
evaluation of six frontier models under a dual-benchmark framework,
paired with a specialized reasoning scaffold designed to navigate
Persian cultural pragmatics.

## 2. Related Work

Prior work relevant to this study spans non-English NLP benchmarking,
emotion detection in Persian text, and dataset contamination mitigation.
A central challenge identified in the literature---particularly
highlighted during the development of corpora like ArmanEmo and
EmoPars---is managing highly imbalanced emotion classes and cross-domain
variance (e.g., formal news versus informal Twitter/Instagram comments).

Despite the introduction of these localized datasets, many general
evaluations continue to apply simplified, single-step prompting to
complex socio-cultural tasks. Furthermore, while the NLP community has
extensively analyzed intra-family model performance on Western
languages, controlled inter-model benchmarking that accounts for the
memorization of non-English public datasets remains comparatively
underexplored. This study addresses this gap by establishing
domain-aligned classification metrics alongside an uncontaminated
zero-day benchmark suite and an explicit socio-linguistic reasoning
methodology.

## 3. Data

We evaluated model performance across 713 total Persian social media
instances partitioned into two distinct evaluation sets:

- **Public Baseline (ArmanEmo-Core):** A curated 356-instance subset
  derived from the public ArmanEmo dataset. This set contains social
  media text mapped to Ekman's six basic emotions (Anger, Fear,
  Happiness, Hatred, Sadness, Wonder) plus an \"Other\" category,
  serving to measure baseline capability and potential pre-training
  memorization.

- **Zero-Day Evaluation (PersianEmo-ZeroDay):** A novel, sequestered
  dataset of 357 multi-platform Persian social media posts collected
  strictly post-knowledge-cutoff. These instances were explicitly
  selected by native linguists to feature complex cultural phenomena
  (e.g., *Ta\'arof*, regional idioms, heavy slang, and complex sarcastic
  structures) to mimic unmemorizable, authentic linguistic scenarios.

## 4. Methodology

We compared six state-of-the-art language models: GPT-5.4 Pro, Gemini
3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen-2.5, DeepSeek-v3, and Grok 4. All
models were evaluated under identical zero-shot prompt configurations
and deterministic decoding settings (T = 0.0). Models were tested under
two operational paradigms: Direct Generation and **Socio-Linguistic
Emotion Chain-of-Thought (SLE-CoT)**.

SLE-CoT mandates a three-stage structured reasoning trace before the
final emotion label is predicted:

1.  **Lexical & Orthographic Parsing:** Identify and normalize informal
    spellings, stretched letters, transliterations, and local slang.

2.  **Socio-Pragmatic Analysis:** Evaluate the text for implicit
    cultural nuances, checking for sarcasm, irony, or politeness
    protocols (*Ta\'arof*) that might invert the literal meaning.

3.  **Final Emotion Classification:** Synthesize the normalized text and
    pragmatic context to output a single definitive Ekman emotion label.

Rather than candidate retrieval metrics, performance was evaluated using
task-aligned classification indicators:

- **Macro-F1 (M-F1):** The unweighted mean of the F1-scores calculated
  for each emotion class, penalizing models that only perform well on
  majority classes (like Happiness or Sadness) while failing on minority
  classes (like Fear or Wonder).

- **Emotion Classification Accuracy (ECA):** The strict accuracy of
  predicting the exact ground-truth emotion label.

- **Cultural Pragmatic Alignment (CPA):** A binary metric tracking
  whether the model correctly identifies the underlying sentiment in
  text containing sarcasm or *Ta\'arof*, rather than defaulting to
  literal translation.

Statistical differences were assessed using an omnibus Cochran's Q test
on binarized task success indicators, followed by post-hoc pairwise
McNemar's tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction (α = 0.05). Effect sizes
were calculated via Cohen's w, with 95% confidence intervals estimated
via non-parametric bootstrapping with 1,000 iterations.

## 5. Results

Performance varied significantly across models, reasoning paradigms, and
dataset contamination levels. On the public ArmanEmo-Core baseline,
GPT-5.4 Pro achieved the highest direct Emotion Classification Accuracy
(ECA = 0.86) and Macro-F1 (0.83). However, on the uncontaminated
PersianEmo-ZeroDay dataset, all models exhibited uniform performance
degradation. GPT-5.4 Pro\'s direct ECA dropped to 0.69, while competing
models displayed relative performance drops ranging from 17% to 24%.

An omnibus Cochran\'s Q test on the zero-day benchmark confirmed
statistically significant performance differences across the six models
(Cochran\'s Q(5) = 42.18, p \< .001, Cohen\'s w = 0.25). Post-hoc
pairwise McNemar's tests with Holm correction indicated that GPT-5.4 Pro
maintained a statistically significant lead over the remaining models
under unseen conditions (adjusted p \< .05). Following GPT-5.4 Pro,
Claude Sonnet 4.5 (0.66 ECA) and Qwen-2.5 (0.64 ECA) demonstrated the
strongest localized Persian comprehension, whereas models with less
multilingual pre-training struggled heavily with informal orthography.

The deployment of the **SLE-CoT** reasoning scaffold yielded
statistically significant performance recoveries across all six models
on the zero-day evaluation suite. GPT-5.4 Pro\'s ECA improved from 0.69
to 0.83, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 climbed from 0.66 to 0.80. On average,
Macro-F1 across all models saw a +0.13 lift under SLE-CoT. Error
analysis revealed that under direct generation, models frequently
suffered from \"literal translation bias\" (misclassifying *Ta\'arof*
expressions of extreme humility as \"Sadness\" or \"Fear\"); SLE-CoT\'s
explicit socio-pragmatic mapping phase effectively mitigated these
cultural alignment failures, boosting average CPA by 18.5%.

## 6. Discussion

The experimental findings demonstrate that while leading LLMs possess
strong multilingual capabilities, their baseline emotion detection
performance on public datasets like ArmanEmo is heavily inflated by
dataset memorization. The uniform 17--24% performance drop observed on
PersianEmo-ZeroDay highlights the necessity of using sequestered,
culturally complex evaluation suites when assessing true natural
language understanding in non-English contexts.

Furthermore, the success of Socio-Linguistic Emotion Chain-of-Thought
(SLE-CoT) proves that emotion detection in morphologically rich,
culturally nuanced languages requires structured parsing. Without
reasoning scaffolds, autoregressive generation defaults to literal,
surface-level translation, missing vital subtext. Forcing models to
explicitly normalize informal orthography and audit pragmatic context
prior to classification ensures significantly higher fidelity to
human-annotated ground truths.

Several limitations should be noted. The evaluation utilized fixed
inference settings (T = 0.0), which may limit the models\' ability to
generate diverse linguistic hypotheses. Additionally, the dataset was
restricted to text, omitting multimodal cues (e.g., emojis, attached
images, or audio tone) that are heavily utilized in Persian social media
to convey emotion. Future work should extend this framework to
multimodal Arabic-script languages and explore multi-agent debate for
resolving highly ambiguous sarcastic posts. Overall, this study provides
a standardized, contamination-resistant baseline for evaluating LLMs on
Persian emotion detection.
